# Campground Curation (inclusion criteria & the state-by-state sweep)

Split out of CLAUDE.md, which keeps the *runtime* half of the campground system
(the two location files, id-based references, the delete guard, the evidence-field
strip). This file is the **data** half: what earns an entry in the location database,
how `waterfront` is proven, and the per-state sweep pipeline that adds and audits them.

**Everything here is load-bearing and most of it corrects a specific mistake that was
made once.** Read this file before adding, editing, removing or auditing any entry in
`campgrounds.json` — including a single one-off add. The rules are deliberately
asymmetric (absence of confirmation always resolves *down*), which is not something a
reasonable person would guess.

## Campground Inclusion Criteria

Entries must be a real, publicly-bookable, drive-in RV campground usable by the family's 23-ft RV ("EKKO"). Before adding one:
- **Access:** drive-in, passable by a normal vehicle (decent dirt/gravel is fine; hardcore offroad / 4WD-required disqualifies). No pad needed (grass is fine); hookups never required.
- **RV size:** at least some drive-in sites must fit a 23-ft rig. If the largest sites cap around 20 ft, exclude — EKKO can occasionally squeeze into a 20-ft spot but we don't rely on it (barring a genuinely special case).
  - **Rig length from reviews (AWH 2026-10-22).** A reviewer's rig length never goes into `sites.max_rig_ft` — one rig fitting is not a limit, and the field would read as the agency's. What the agency says decides how much a review can do: **silent on length** → keep, and when a review from the last 3 years says the reviewer's OWN rig of 23 ft or more fit, add it to the note as a dated, attributed caveat ("A 2025 Campendium reviewer reports fitting a 30-ft trailer; the Forest Service gives no length. --Claude"); **vague** ("small trailers only", "limited access for trailers and RV's") → such a review settles it as a keep, and with none the entry is dropped (Arod Lake FAS 14104, Ekalaka Park 6325); **explicit** ("motorhomes / large RVs not recommended", a 4WD or high-clearance requirement or recommendation) → dropped whatever reviews say, since one driver getting through doesn't make the road fit (Grizzly 6227, Bitterroot Flat 6308, Dalles 6320).
- **Never add:** horse/equestrian campgrounds (a general campground that merely *offers* horse sites is OK); hike-in / tent-only / boat-in / cabin-only / group-only; fairgrounds that only allow camping during events. **Off-road parks and OHV campgrounds** (AWH 2026-10-02: not in scope) — any owner: private ATV/ORV/mud/motocross parks, county OHV parks, state vehicular recreation areas, BLM open OHV areas, and agency campgrounds that exist to stage riders (named OHV/ORV/ATV, or a trailhead camp inside an OHV system). A general campground that merely *has* an OHV side — a lake campground that allows ORVs, a provincial recreation area where OHV staging is one use, an RV park on an ATV trail — stays, like the horse-sites case. 58 removed under this rule; per-id reasons, and the 14 judged general and kept, in `audit/offroad_scope/decisions.jsonl`.
- **Not a real transient campground — exclude even if it technically takes overnighters:** parks that are mostly seasonal/full-timer, residential / mobile-home-park, or workforce/long-term (e.g. gas-field worker housing); membership / club / sales-pitch parks (Thousand Trails, Encore, Bluegreen, Coast-to-Coast; Elks/Moose/Airstream-club lodges; timeshare "buy-in" resorts); casino/racetrack overnight lots; truck-stop RV hookup pads (e.g. Love's RV Hookups, even when bookable with full hookups — AWH 2026-09-30).
- **A standing minimum stay is kept if it is clearly marked** (AWH 2026-10-10: "from now on all campgrounds with a 2-night minimum can stay as long as it is clearly marked as such"; this replaced the 2026-10-01 rule that disqualified any standing 2+ night minimum). Mark it in `booking.min_stay` (with `applies` / `season`), `booking.note` and `provenance.booking` (source and date), and append a dated `MINIMUM STAY: N nights ...` line to the note. 3-night standing minimums have been kept the same way. Every longer one that covers ordinary summer nights was dropped, so raise such a minimum as a likely drop: Nickerson Beach (1447, 7 nights Memorial Day - Labor Day, dropped 2026-10-11), Alcona Park (3996, 5 nights June 25 - August 15, dropped 2026-10-12) and American Way RV Park WV (4 nights), left out the same day. A long minimum confined to a holiday is fine (AWH 2026-10-12: fine "as long as those long minimums don't apply to regular days throughout the summer"; Paradise Shores CA, 5 nights over the 4th of July only). Read what a minimum is attached to before raising it: The Landing on Red Gut Bay was dropped for a "4 Day minimum charge" and restored as 15360 the same day, when the rates page archived in 2023 showed the line under its cabin-rates heading. Weekend and holiday minimums are fine, as before. Whether a minimum is standing is judged by whether a single night is actually sold, not by the policy page: a late-booking waiver, a phone exception, a same-day single-night product or walk-up/FCFS single nights all mean it is NOT standing. SC state parks publish a year-round 2-night minimum but sell single nights by phone inside a week (AWH, trips 24/56/88); Go Camp Tennessee says "call to request an exception" for one night. The entries removed under the old rule on 2026-10-01: `audit/minimum_stay_removals_2026-10-01.json`.
- **No live web presence is a DISQUALIFIER, not just a strike** (AWH 2026-09-08). A campground needs at least one of: a working official website carrying photos, current prices and a phone number, OR an active bookable listing on a platform (Hipcamp `isBookable`, recreation.gov/ReserveAmerica/US-eDirect, Campspot, RoverPass with the listing actually **claimed**). A park with neither is excluded even when it demonstrably operates and reviews well — "a Hipcamp listing or a simple website with photos, current prices and an active phone number are not difficult to maintain if you are running a serious business," and planning around one is a hassle not worth the entry. Judge the presence as **live**, not merely existing: a 404 or a domain that now redirects elsewhere is dead; a Hipcamp listing at `status: ASLEEP` / `isBookable: false` is dormant; an **unclaimed** aggregator listing is a directory record someone else wrote, not the operator's presence — look for "Claim my Campground", a stale "Last Updated", and auto-generated filler (Lee Hi's named Colonial Williamsburg and Busch Gardens, ~3 hrs away, as nearby attractions). Removed under this rule: Lee Hi Travel Plaza (VA, was 13096), Interstate Campground (VA, was 13097), Montrose Campground (PA, was 13115).
  - **Reading a RoverPass page** (2026-10-01): claimed = an **Instant Book** or **Request to Book** button. "Claim my Campground" sits in every page's footer, so it proves nothing, and an unclaimed page can still show imported per-site rates (Dobbins RV Park) — a price on the page is not the signal. `audit/aggregator_url/` has the probe.
  - **A Google listing can stand in for a website (AWH 2026-10-01 amendment, reaffirmed 2026-10-14):** "a Google maps review of a park that is open with a phone and 25+ reviews can be used to add or keep a park in the absence of a website." The Google Maps place must sit at our pin (within ~2 km), not be marked permanently or temporarily closed, carry a phone, and have **25+ reviews**; write it into `inclusion_evidence` with the date. A phone number alone proves nothing: directories keep dead numbers for years. History: 46 kept on it in the 10-01 aggregator-URL cleanup (8 closed ones removed: 3883, 5300, 6992, 7881, 8071, 8130, 8723, 8781). On 2026-10-14 the 78 private entries standing only on it were dropped on a reading of #5 that AWH corrected the same day; they were restored (the revert of 211ab03). Of the other 17 no-web-presence drops that day, which never had a Google check, five passed it and were restored (Camp Morning Wood 1204, Reddmann's 3887, River Road 3910, Triangle 7877, Red Ledge 10160; `roverpass_ops_2026-10-14/google_restore_apply.py`). Stephenville 7826 failed: Google's listing of that name is a different park 2.3 km away.
  - **The Good Sam discount counts toward the price gate** (AWH 2026-10-14: "I have a Good Sam membership and qualify for a Good Sam discount at participating parks. This means a park may pass the price gate on this discount."). At a Good Sam Park (`discounts.good_sam: true`, i.e. `isGsPark` in the directory) test the park's own posted Good Sam rate, else its rate less 10%, against US$50 / C$69. `fees` still records the undiscounted rate; the discount lives in `discounts`. Re-testing the 42 earlier price drops restored four (Triple J 2227, Gulf Breeze 4943, Bayou Bend 7385, The Palms 7860; `audit/price_research/goodsam_gate_2026-10-14/`); Fisherman's Cove 12655, Nevada Treasure 11305 and Riverbend West 1519 are still over.
  - **The private-campground rule in one place (AWH 2026-10-14, restated): two indicators, both required.** A **working booking page** satisfies both. Otherwise **price** = a published price on a working website, or an RV Life `$`/`$$`; **presence** = a working website with a phone number (Facebook is not a website), or a Google Maps listing showing an open business with 25+ reviews and a phone. The indicators are evidence, not the price gate: a published rate over US$50 / C$69 still drops unless a gateway applies, and a long-stay park still needs a posted nightly rate or a booking site (test 2 below). A Firefly page whose rate sits behind its captcha counts as a working booking page (a captcha is not a login). Town and county parks are exempt.
  - **A posted rate is not required when RV Life rates the park `$` or `$$`** (AWH 2026-10-14: "an RV Life $ or $$ can be used to add or keep a park in the absence of a published rate"). So #5's "published prices on regular website and phone number" is one way in, not the only one: a park with its own website and a phone, or a qualifying Google listing, but no posted rate stays on an RV Life `$`/`$$`. The 22 `hold="b"` parks of the RoverPass operator pass and Liberty Haven 11558 were kept on it. A long-stay park is the exception (next bullet): it needs a posted nightly rate or a booking site.
  - **Long-stay, seasonal and residential parks that also sell nights** (AWH 2026-10-14, the #2 criteria). A private park that mostly sells months or seasons, or houses residents, stays only if it passes all five tests: (1) **price**: at most US$50 / C$69 before tax unless a gateway or named exception applies; (2) **nights really for sale**: a nightly rate on its own website plus a phone, or a booking site that sells one night, and no application, approval, background check, lease, waitlist, membership or photos of the rig (Geronimo 7537, The Hideaway 7856); an RV Life tier does not stand in here; (3) **no minimum over 2 nights**, except on holiday weekends; "monthly only", or an announced switch to it, fails (Ledgestone 7627, Vorwaller 10188); (4) **at least 1 site in 10 sold by the night**, when the park, its site map or its booking site gives a count; no count anywhere passes (Sutter's 9% and Wanna Ruf It skipped, Plage Bernard 10% kept, Blue Water 1312 dropped at 12 of 209); (5) **an RV park, not a home community**: it fails if it sells homes or lots or its mobile/manufactured homes outnumber its RV sites; monthly residents in RVs, work crews, park models or a smaller mobile-home section don't fail it on their own (Kountry Ranch 7600 kept; Sunny Pines 14426 and The Preserve 7861 dropped). A kept park's note says what it mostly is ("Mostly seasonal: 20 of its 160 sites are sold by the night"); 55+ and adults-only stay a note line. Applied 2026-10-14 (`roverpass_ops_2026-10-14/awh_answers_apply.py`): 26 dropped, including Tymber 1224 and Clemson Hills 4377, whose 10-13 keeps AWH reversed.
  - **Rates or availability behind a login** (AWH 2026-10-11: "drop anything that requires an account or login"): a login needed just to see rates or availability fails; one needed only at checkout (recreation.gov, ReserveAmerica) is fine, and a captcha is not a login. Jetty Park 13374 and Red Rock Park 13594 were dropped on it. **Softened 2026-10-14 (#3):** a park whose booking calendar needs a login stays if its own website (not Facebook) publishes its prices and a phone number. Nine were kept on it: Central State Park 6829 and Scissortail 6834 (Edmond OK), Hells Canyon 9615, Linn County's Whitcomb Creek 11014, River Bend 11033, Cascadia 11083 and Edgewater 11084, Cresap Bay 10515 and Crook County RV Park 11073.
  - **Town and county parks are exempt from the removal half** (AWH 2026-10-01): use the town's or county's own page when one exists; when the town has none, the entry keeps its aggregator link. A municipal FCFS park isn't a business that can be expected to maintain a listing.
  - **Public campgrounds with no agency page (AWH 2026-10-22).** The private rule (price + presence, 25 Google reviews) answers whether a business still sells nights; a public campground's existence rests on its agency, so it gets its own ladder, checked in order: (1) **an agency or town page, or an agency document, names it as a campground** — a lake-wide fee table, a facilities PDF or a forest order counts, and the managing office's phone is fine; (2) **an official tourism website describes it in detail** — a state tourism office (visitmt.com) or a government-designated tourism region / county or city visitor bureau, not a commercial directory; give its URL as the website; (3) **Google Maps** lists it as a campground with a phone (keep, with a note to call that number to confirm it is open before going: Sanford Park 6092), or a review from the last two years confirms overnight camping, however few reviews there are (Chester City Park 6118, 2 reviews; Barretts Park 6193; Ed McGivern 13577); (4) **none of these** → drop for lack of information (Tri-County Fairgrounds 6113, whose reviews are all rodeos and 4-H). Two exceptions: a **club- or nonprofit-run** park (Kiwanis 6159) is not public and takes the private rule; a **dispersed** area still needs an agency designation, never a Google listing (Homestake Pass 6071). Public entries have no price gate and no published-rate requirement.
- **Unrated / thinly-reviewed needs firsthand confirmation, not a guess.** An RV Life `star_rating` of 0 means *unrated*, and few/no reviews — not automatically excluded (public/government sites are legitimately lightly reviewed), but it removes your safety net. Before adding such an entry you must confirm the basics — fee/reservation model, drive-in RV access, RV-fitting site length, amenities, currently-operating — from a firsthand or authoritative source (official/agency page, campground map/photos, a dated trip report, or a directory with real specifics). If you can't confirm those facts, **skip it** rather than inserting a placeholder you'll have to walk back.
- **Every `note` claim must be sourced — never copy aggregator boilerplate.** Listing aggregators (snoflo, camping.org, campingroadtrip, mobilerving, etc.) auto-generate generic prose like "clean restrooms, hot showers, picnic tables, fire pits… reservations are required… popular among campers." That is filler, not data — do not lift it into a `note`. Prefer the operator's own page, the reservation system, campground photos/site maps, and firsthand reports. When an aggregator's prose disagrees with its own structured fields (e.g. prose says "reservations required" but the data field says "Reservations: No"), the structured field wins.
- **First-come-first-served:** always add a `note` saying so when there's no reservation system. **Consistency gate:** if a `note` asserts "reservations required," the record must carry a real booking channel — a reservation `website` (recreation.gov / reserveamerica / a state/agency portal) or a verified `phone`. A "reservations required" claim with no booking channel is self-contradictory and almost always wrong (it's usually a free FCFS site); resolve it before inserting, don't ship the contradiction. Many USACE / federal river and reservoir areas are free, primitive, and FCFS by default — assume FCFS unless a booking channel actually exists.
- **Dispersed sites:** drive-in dispersed sites may be added when encountered, but don't go out of the way to hunt them.
- **Ownership values:** `state` (state parks, state forests/SRAs, state-DNR areas, and interstate-compact parks like Breaks), `wma`, `federal` (USACE / USFS / NPS / BLM / Reclamation), `local` (county / town / city / regional or municipal authority), `private`, `hipcamp`. State-forest campgrounds are `state`, distinct from state parks. County land run by a private concessionaire is still `local`; a utility-run campground (e.g. Brookfield/PPL) is `private`.
  - **`hipcamp` is a host property whose camping exists only as a listing** — a farm, ranch or homestead with no web presence of its own beyond Hipcamp (or a peer platform like Campertunity). Ownership names who RUNS the campground, never where you book it: **an operator with a genuine website of its own is `private`** (or `local`, for a city/county park) **even when Hipcamp is its only way to book**, and the channel goes in `booking.platform: "hipcamp"` (AWH 2026-10-05; eight moved on this rule: 462, 671, 757, 1988, 2636, 2858, 11558, 14372). A Facebook page or an aggregator listing is not a website of its own. The value matters beyond labelling: `hipcamp` starts unchecked in the map's Ownership filter, so filing a real RV park under it hides the park by default.
  - **`wma` is a state wildlife-agency tract where the camping is INFORMAL** — a WMA / SWA / WHMA / wildlife area on which you may overnight in a parking area, pull-off or undesignated clearing, usually with a free permit or a hunting/fishing license, and nothing that resembles a campground. The land is state-owned, so the value is not really about ownership; it is its own value because that is what gives it a checkbox in the map's Ownership filter, and hiding the class in one click is the whole point (a WMA parking lot is not an alternative to a state park). **The split is by the CAMPING, not the landlord:** a wildlife area running a real campground — numbered sites, hookups, reservations — stays `state` (most Illinois State Fish & Wildlife Areas, CO's San Luis Lakes SWA, NV's Dave Deacon Campground, FL's Dinner Island Ranch WMA camps). Federal equivalents (NWRs, USFS hunter camps on a WMA) stay `federal` — the label is "State WMA" and the informal-camping distinction there is already carried by the note.
- **Waterfront reflects RV-accessible sites only, and an on-water value must be *earned* with site-level proof.** Riverfront/lakefront cabins or tent-only/shelter sites do NOT qualify. The standard is a burden of proof, not a vibe — follow it exactly:
  - **Default down.** The value is `not waterfront` unless positive, named evidence upgrades it. "Couldn't confirm" always resolves *down* (to `*view` or `not waterfront`), never up. Absence of confirmation is never permission.
  - **Evidence that COUNTS** (any one promotes to an on-water value): an official per-site campground map/PDF showing pads at the shoreline; a rec.gov per-site `SHORELINE SITE: Y` flag; **satellite imagery at the pinned campground coordinate showing RV pads within ~1 pad-depth of the waterline**; or a dated firsthand photo of an RV site on the water.
  - **An open, unused apron to the water counts as on-water — the discriminator is a real *buffer*, not the ground cover.** What earns `lakefront`/`riverfront` is a pad with an open, unobstructed, walkable apron to the water — and that apron may be **sand, dune, OR grass**. Sand vs. grass is irrelevant: a pad sitting on an open sandy beach and a pad sitting on an open unused lawn that runs to the shore are the same case (a camper can walk an unobstructed path and put a boat in). The apron's **width does NOT downgrade**, and at a reservoir neither does drawdown — measure to the **beach/high-water edge, not the drawn-down waterline** — SUBJECT TO the distance bound below. Scattered trees that don't meaningfully block view or access do not disqualify. What DOES disqualify (a genuine separating **buffer**): a continuous treeline/vegetated screen; a developed or *used/populated* strip between site and water (road / parking / day-use beach lawn / picnic ground / cabins — "unused" is the operative word in the apron test); or an elevated **bluff/bench** the site sits atop with a graded slope down (climb-down, not walk-across). Those resolve to `lakeview` if the open view holds, else `not waterfront`.
    - **Distance bound (on-water vs `*view`):** the open apron earns on-water only when it is roughly the **campground's own frontage — within ~50 m / a site-depth or two** of the water (the functional test: a short, unobstructed walk you'd actually carry a kayak across). An open, visible, but genuinely *distant* span (e.g. ~150–250 m across a field) is a **view, not access** → `*view`. Don't let "open grass" swallow `lakeview`; that tier is exactly for set-back-but-visible sites.
    - (This generalized a rule that first only counted sand — which had corrected a CO sweep reading low-pool tiles as a wide beach "buffer," e.g. Jackson Lake — to the equivalent open-grass apron, per AWH 2026-06-14: ground cover is arbitrary, the buffer/use and the distance are what matter.)
    - **COASTAL special case — `coastal dunes` / `coastal woods`:** the one override of the dune/treeline-buffer + distance rules above. A campground sitting behind an **undeveloped shore dune field** (`coastal dunes`) or, on a Great Lake/ocean, set back in the **shore forest** (`coastal woods`) is **on-water even with NO line of sight and well beyond ~50 m**, PROVIDED the land between sites and water is the campground's **own undeveloped dune/forest with no other USE** (campers walk/boardwalk through it to the beach). The dune/woods is the coastal setting itself, not a buffer.
      - **`coastal dunes` applies on ANY body of water large enough to have built a genuine wind-formed dune field** — ocean, Great Lake, OR a large inland lake (Lake Winnipeg, Lake Manitoba, Great Salt Lake, etc.). Dunes only form where a big water body supplies enough fetch/wind/sand, so "large enough to have real dunes" is the self-limiting gate. The discriminator is a **genuine, substantial dune field** — satellite-confirmed hummocky/vegetated wind dunes (e.g. Grand Beach on Lake Winnipeg) — NOT merely a flat sandy beach, a single low foredune berm, or a mowed picnic lawn. A small pond or an ordinary lake beach with no true dune field does NOT qualify; judge those by the standard open-apron/buffer + distance rules (→ `lakefront`/`lakeview`/`not waterfront`). (AWH 2026-07-14: extended coastal dunes from Great-Lakes/ocean-only to any dune-building water body, correcting the MB sweep that wrongly downgraded dune-backed Lake Winnipeg parks.)
      - **`coastal woods` stays Great Lakes & ocean ONLY** (never inland water) — a set-back-in-the-forest, no-sightline call has no dune-field physical gate, so it's reserved for true marine / Great-Lakes shore forest.
      - STILL disqualifies for both (→ `*view`/`not waterfront`): a **road/highway** between camp and shore (US-41/US-31/M-25/PTH), a developed **day-use beach + parking complex**, a **cabin/residential strip**, or the loop genuinely inland with other land use between — those are real *uses*. Satellite look still mandatory (confirm the gap is the campground's own undeveloped dune/woods — not a road/lot/day-use strip — and for the inland case that the dunes are genuine wind dunes, not just beach sand); canopy/dune-blind + agency "on Lake <X> / beach access" supports it. The D.H. Day / Warren Dunes / Hoffmaster / Sleeping Bear / Pictured Rocks / Grand Beach case. (Originally AWH 2026-06-15, after a MI state sweep wrongly downgraded dune-backed Great-Lakes parks to not-waterfront.)
  - **Below the dam is not on the lake (AWH 2026-10-21).** At a reservoir or dammed lake, a campground that sits **behind the dam's berm/embankment, on the downstream side of the dam, or below the lake's elevation** gets no lake value — neither `lakefront` nor `lakeview` — because the berm blocks both the walk to the water and the sightline. Overhead imagery flattens this: a pad can look 30 m from open water across what is really an earthen embankment several metres high, so a "legible" satellite frame does NOT settle it. Whenever a lake call is near a dam: find the dam, and compare the campground's DEM elevation (Open-Meteo `/v1/elevation`, the same source as `elevation_meters`) with the lake's normal pool (the agency's stated pool elevation, else the DEM on the open water). A campground meaningfully below pool (more than a few metres) or downstream of the dam axis is judged only against the water it actually sits on — usually the tailwater river (`riverfront`/`riverview`) on its own evidence, else `not waterfront`. Name the check in `waterfront_evidence` ("dam 400 m E; pads 12 m below pool").
  - **Evidence that is FORBIDDEN as the sole basis**: marketing prose ("on the lake," "lakeside resort"); aggregator boilerplate or vague review snippets ("some sites near the water"); a brochure *overview* map (an icon merely *near* water); the campground's **name** ("Lakeview"/"Creekside"/"Riverbend"); or the mere presence of "lake/river access," a boat ramp, or a marina. A marina park whose pads sit uphill is `lakeview`/`not waterfront`, not `lakefront`.
  - **A claim that disagrees with ours is a lead for a re-audit, in either direction** (AWH 2026-10-14, #7). When a booking engine sells a water-named site type ("Waterfront Site", "Lakeside", "Canal Spaces"), an operator's map or page puts sites on the water, or either one contradicts an on-water call, re-run the gate on that campground; the claim itself never decides. Sources that settled the 2026-10-14 re-audits (`audit/price_research/waterfront_reaudit_2026-10-14/` and `roverpass_ops_2026-10-14/waterfront_reaudit.py`): the operator's Google My Maps exported as KML (`https://www.google.com/maps/d/kml?mid=<id>&forcekml=1`, per-site points), the operator's own aerial photos, a booking engine's per-site map, the coordinates in a page's schema.org block or a RoverPass record (for a pin that sits off the campground), and in North Carolina Esri Wayback's NC911 release (0.15 m, leaf-off).
  - **The satellite look is MANDATORY but its authority is ASYMMETRIC — it can veto, but it cannot be the sole confirmer in canopy.** Always pull the coordinate and look, but weigh it correctly: (a) when the imagery is *legible*, it is decisive and **overrides prose/brochure maps** — pads visibly set back from the water → downgrade (this is where it catches marinas, brochure-map pins, and "lake access" parks) — but an open unused **apron (sand, dune, or grass) within the distance bound** in front of the pad is NOT "set back" (see the open-apron rule above): a downgrade needs a real buffer (treeline, developed/used strip, or a bluff/bench the site sits atop), not just open sand/grass or a drawn-down waterline; (b) when the imagery is *illegible* — dense forest canopy, low-resolution/old base tiles, deep shade (common for Adirondack/boreal/remote sites where individual pads simply can't be resolved) — it can neither confirm nor deny, and "I couldn't see pads" is **NOT** grounds to downgrade. In that case defer to the higher-authority source: an **official per-site campground map** or **per-site reservation/`waterfront` data** is primary and a canopy-blind satellite does not override it. For established agency campgrounds (DEC/COE/USFS/NPS/state parks) that official per-site map almost always exists and is the authority — satellite is the cross-check, not the verdict. Still required: SOME qualifying source (legible-and-positive satellite, OR official per-site map, OR per-site reservation flag, OR dated firsthand photo) must confirm shoreline sites; if none does, default down.
  - **`*view` needs the same rigor** — only if some **RV** sites actually overlook water (confirmed, not assumed); else `not waterfront`. But note the **floor**: a pad on open ground (grass or beach) with the water *visible and unscreened* across that ground is at LEAST `*view` — it cannot be `not waterfront`, because nothing on open ground blocks the sightline. `not waterfront` for a water-adjacent site requires a real screen/buffer (treeline, building, berm), the site being genuinely far/inland, or the water not visible — not merely "set back across open grass."
  - **Record the proof in the `waterfront_evidence` JSON field.** Any on-water (or `*view`) designation must carry a one-line `waterfront_evidence` string on the entry itself, naming the artifact (e.g. "rec.gov site map: sites 12–15 on shore" / "satellite at 36.57,-93.30: pads at waterline"). In agent workflows this is a required structured-output field; a designation with no evidence string is written as `not waterfront`. **The field is the SINGLE audit record (NOT the commit message — that proved too easy to skip, GA 2026-06-17):** a non-empty `waterfront_evidence` means the entry has been waterfront-audited; an empty/absent field means it has NOT been audited. That one check is the whole answer — no need to also scan the `note`. Firsthand owner audits (`--AWH` in `note`) were backfilled with a `waterfront_evidence` string on 2026-06-17 (capturing the owner's pre-`--AWH` note text), so they too carry the field. **Keep this invariant:** whenever you record a firsthand `--AWH` waterfront call, also write a `waterfront_evidence` string, so the field stays the sole source of truth. The `apply_waterfront_audit.py` script writes the field from each result's `evidence`; keep a copy of the evidence in the commit message too for convenient `git log` grepping, but the JSON field is the source of truth.

## Campground Data Curation (state-by-state audit)

An ongoing effort adds campgrounds missing from `campgrounds.json`, worked systematically by state and by ownership/operator. **Git log is the progress record** — read recent commits to see which states/categories are done before continuing. Typical order per state: state (parks → forests/SRAs → WMAs, which take `ownership: wma` when their camping is informal) → federal (USACE / USFS / NPS) → private → local. (State + federal are largely done for VA / MD / DE / PA / WV.)

**Waterfront audit is a built-in sweep stage, not a separate pass.** The original pre-gate re-audit (re-verifying old `waterfront` values state by state) is **COMPLETE**. For every new state from here on, fold the audit's full strength into the sweep so entries land correct the first time (chosen approach, KS-style 2026-06-11). Per-state pipeline:
  1. detect (RV Life) → vet inclusion criteria → pin `location` → fetch elevation. While pinning, the research agent captures a structured waterfront **`lead`** — `{map_url, water_body, candidate_sites, note}`, the per-site campground map it already found plus any site numbers it saw near the water — and returns it. The lead is a head start for the audit, NOT a verdict (don't let it set `waterfront`);
  2. write the candidate entries with `waterfront: "not waterfront"` as a placeholder (don't guess from name/marketing/lead) **and an `inclusion_evidence` string capturing the add-time validity proof** (the operator/reservation-system source confirming it's a real drive-in RV campground — see the inclusion-audit paragraph below);
  3. run the **same** `audit/waterfront_audit_instructions.md` subagents over the just-added ids (~8 ids per batch), **carrying each entry's `lead` into its batch object** so the audit verifies the already-found per-site map instead of re-discovering it (seam optimization, AWH 2026-06-14) — the satellite look stays mandatory and the gate still decides; satellite + per-site-map/rec.gov check + coord verification happen here at full strength. **Run the audit batches SEQUENTIALLY, one subagent at a time** (default for every sweep stage): parallel batches exhaust the session limit and lose in-flight work — only fan out in parallel on an explicit per-stage user OK (GA 2026-07-15, after a parallel WA-audit wave hit the session limit twice);
  4. apply with `python3 audit/apply_waterfront_audit.py <results.json>` (sets `waterfront`, fixes mis-pinned `location`/`elevation_meters`, **and writes each entry's `waterfront_evidence` from the result's `evidence` string**);
  5. the `waterfront_evidence` JSON field is the durable audit record (empty/absent = not audited; `--AWH` note = audited). Echo the per-entry evidence in the commit message too ("Audit all NN <ST> waterfront designations") for convenient `git log` grepping, but the field is the source of truth — do not rely on the commit alone.
  Methodology, subagent instructions, and apply script live in `audit/` (see `audit/README.md`). Skip the satellite look only for entries with no water anywhere near (pure in-town RV parks, fairgrounds) — they stay `not waterfront` by default-down. You may run the audit as its own commit right after the add commit, or thread it into the add; either way it is mandatory before a state is "done."

**Inclusion (validity) audit is a built-in sweep stage too — recorded at ADD time, not a separate pass** (standard since 2026-06-25, after a cabins-only park, Canoe Creek SP, was found to have slipped into the DB from an early less-rigorous import). It verifies the entry is a *real, currently-operating, drive-in RV campground fitting a 23-ft rig* — catching cabins-only / tent-only / hike-in-boat-in / group-only / day-use-only / fairground-event-only / membership / residential-seasonal / closed / under-23ft / duplicate entries. Unlike the waterfront audit it does NOT need a separate subagent pass during a sweep: the add-stage research agent is **already the authority** that decides keep/drop (it reads the operator/agency page + reservation-system per-site list to vet inclusion in pipeline step 1), so it simply **emits a one-line `inclusion_evidence` string for each entry it keeps** and writes it into the entry (pipeline step 2). The `inclusion_evidence` JSON field is the durable record: **non-empty == inclusion-audited & confirmed a valid campground; empty/absent == NOT audited** (same invariant as `waterfront_evidence`). Discipline: the proof must name an **authoritative source** (DCNR/agency "Stay" page, ReserveAmerica/recreation.gov per-site site-type list, the operator's own site) — **aggregators (snoflo, camperalerts, campscanner, thedyrt/camping.org summaries) routinely INFLATE cabin-only/day-use parks into fake "RV sites"; never confirm a keep from an aggregator alone**, and when an aggregator conflicts with the operator the operator wins. For **retroactive** re-vetting of a state added before this discipline (PA was the completed pilot — 169 entries, 11 removed), run the separate `audit/inclusion_audit_instructions.md` subagents and apply with `python3 audit/apply_inclusion_audit.py <results.json>` — it stamps `inclusion_evidence` on confirmed keeps and **reports** the `remove`/`review` candidates without auto-deleting (a human reviews the remove list before excising the `{ }` block by id; check `trip_data/` for `campground_id` references first).

**Detection data sources (RV Life is the workhorse):**
- **RV Life** (`campgrounds.rvlife.com`) — Algolia app `H0LPZK92QJ` (search key is inline in any park page's HTML: `algoliasearch('H0LPZK92QJ', '<key>')`), index `park`. Useful fields: `price_level` (int 0–4 = number of `$` signs), `star_rating` (0–5 user rating — note `avg_rate` is the nightly price, NOT a rating), `park_type` (`commercial`/`state`/`county`/`city`/`regional`/`dnr`/`coe`/`usfs`/`national`/`military`), `cg_name`, `city_name`, `region_abbvr`, `_geoloc`. `region_abbvr` is NOT facetable — pull a state via `insideBoundingBox=[[minLat,minLng,maxLat,maxLng]]` then keep `region_abbvr == <ST>`.
- **Good Sam** — Algolia index `gs-ml-cb-assets-prod` (app `VT01MNVCP5`); search key is a 2-hour secret fetched from an AppSync `getCampgroundSearchAuthSecret` query (use Python `urllib` for the POST — the sandbox blocks `curl` POST). Carries triple ratings and a `campground.type` `MEMBERSHIP_PARK` flag (unreliable — confirm membership independently).

**Private (commercial) campgrounds:** add only if the park's **own published base rate is ≤ $50/night** AND it is well regarded (RV Life `star_rating` ≥ 4, or the fallback below) AND not a membership park — **except in a national-park gateway area, where the price half of the gate is lifted** (AWH 2026-09-20); then vet each per the inclusion criteria (real transient RV campground, not seasonal/residential/workforce/club; dead-website strike) and set waterfront from the RV sites via the evidence gate (default `not waterfront`; on-water only with a satellite/site-map confirmation, never from a "lakeside"/"riverfront" name or marketing).
  - **The price gate is RV Life's `$` tag again, for simplicity** (AWH 2026-10-01, reversing the 2026-09-30 published-rate gate). In order: (1) RV Life `price_level` `$` or `$$` passes price with **no check of the actual price**; (2) `$$$` or above is a skip unless a special exception applies (the gateway exception below); (3) no `price_level` -> RV Life `avg_rate` <= $40; (4) no `price_level` and no `avg_rate` -> find a published base rate (cheapest hookup RV site, <= $50) and record it with the date in `inclusion_evidence`. Every other gate (quality, live web presence, transient not seasonal, standing minimum stay, 23-ft fit) still applies. The 2026-09-30 experiment is why the tag is known to be rough: a 34-park check (`audit/private_gap_price_check.jsonl`) found `avg_rate` below the published rate at 21 of 26 parks (median +$8) and in the same `$` tier only 16 of 26 times - AWH accepted that error for a gate that needs no per-park price reading.
  - **Not retroactive** (AWH 2026-10-01): `$$$`+ private entries already in the database stay, including the 69 private-gap adds (ids 14201-14306) vetted under the 2026-09-30 published-rate gate. Each passed the gate in force when it was added, and `rating.price_tier` lets the map's price filter hide them. Don't re-propose a `$$$` cleanup.
  - **Over the limit is a drop unless the park is near a gateway** (AWH 2026-10-14, #4 and the over-limit list: "drop if over price limit unless very close to major gateway points (major cities, major tourist attractions, National Parks, etc.)"). The limit is the base rate (the cheapest RV site a 23-ft rig fits, one night for two, before tax): US$50, or C$69. `audit/price_research/gateways.py` is the gateway test: within 40 km in a straight line of the downtown of a 1M+ metro (US MSA, Canadian CMA), an entrance or main visitor center of a US or Canadian national park, a listed major attraction, or **a family home** (AWH 2026-10-21; read at run time from the gitignored `trip_data/family.json` and reported only as "family home", so no address or name reaches a tracked file). Applied 2026-10-14 to the parks priced in that day's passes (`audit/price_research/awh_calls_2026-10-14.py` and the RoverPass operator pass); Rockwell 14708 ($65, 11 km from downtown Oklahoma City) stayed on it. The named exceptions for Liberty Harbor 3363 and the Fort Myers parks were retired 2026-10-21, since the gateway test now covers them (New York 3 km; family homes). It does not reach the older over-limit entries kept under "Not retroactive" (73 on 2026-10-14; AWH: "leave the existing ones for now").
  - The four-step order replaces "RV Life is the SCREEN, not the gate"; `audit/private_gap_list.py` screens on it.
  - **A `$` with no `avg_rate` is RV Life's DEFAULT, not a rating** - treat it as no tier (step 4: find a published rate). Measured 2026-10-01 over every private-gap candidate: all 144 parks with no `avg_rate` carry `price_level` 1, and no other tier ever appears without a rate; only 69 of the 213 `$` parks have a real one.
  - **A park RV Life doesn't list, or lists unrated, is eligible** on the published rate plus a quality signal from elsewhere — a Good Sam overall (`general`) rating ≥ 8.5 (AWH 2026-09-30), or a Campspot/Google/Campendium/Dyrt average ≥ 4.0 over at least ~25 reviews — together with the firsthand-confirmation rule for thinly reviewed entries above. Before this rule such parks were never candidates (833 Good Sam private parks had no RV Life record, 2026-09-30).
  - **Why 8.5 on Good Sam and not 8** (2026-09-30): across 1,605 parks listed in both directories (MI/FL/TX/PA/NC/WI, 5+ RV Life reviews) the median Good Sam overall score was 8.2 for RV Life 3★, 8.7 for 4★, 9.0 for 4.5★ — Good Sam scores bunch high, so an 8 is a typical 3-star park. It is a weak signal even at 8.5 (15% of the parks it passes would fail RV Life's 4★, against a 24% base rate), so it is the fallback, never a reason to override RV Life.
  - Canada: the published-rate limit is C$69 before tax (the US$50 converted; C$55, used in one report, was an error). RV Life's `$` tags aren't CAD-adjusted; see [[reference_rvlife_price_cad]].
    - **Finding the rate (2026-10-08):** most Canadian parks first logged as "no rate" do post one - on a tarifs page the static crawl can't read (tabs, accordions, an image), in the booking engine, or in last season's archived page. Recipes and tools: `audit/private_gap_browser/README.md` (playbooks at the end). The official Bonjour Québec listing's "prix maximum par nuitée" is operator-supplied and bounds every site: a maximum of C$69 or less passes the price gate (working rule, after the NL newfoundlandlabrador.com precedent). A booking-engine quote overrules a lower posted list price. Quebec campsites are exempt from the 3.5% lodging tax, so a tax-included price divided by 1.14975 is the pre-tax rate.
    - **Mostly seasonal, measured (2026-10-08):** most Quebec private parks are part seasonal; count the traveller share from the operator's map legend, the engine's free sites for a future weeknight, or a pre-opening/post-closing Wayback frame. Working line: traveller sites under ~10% of the park = mostly seasonal, skip (extends AWH's Sutter's ~9% decision); a park with a real traveller block above that is judged on its own.
  - **A campground that is temporarily closed is added, not withheld, and carries `status: {operating: "temporarily_closed", reopens, note}`** in its results row so the map greys it from the first commit (AWH 2026-09-30; schema doc §4.5). A permanent closure is a skip.
  - **A church or ministry camp is fine when its campground is open to the public by the night** (AWH 2026-09-30: Covenant Hills, Winding Creek). The exclusion is a group-only / retreat-only / members-only camp, not a church-run one; say it is ministry-run in the note.
  - **The gateway exception, and why it exists.** In a park-gateway town nearly everything prices at `$$$`, so the price gate empties the map exactly where a traveller needs options. Measured in the Badlands box: 17 RV Life parks, 4 in the database, and FIVE of the misses failed on price alone. The star half of the gate still applies (`star_rating` >= 4), every entry is still vetted against the inclusion criteria one by one, and the `$$$`/`$$$$` tier is recorded in `rating.price_tier` so the map's price filter still hides them for anyone who set one. Applied 2026-09-20 to the Badlands (Interior/Wall/Quinn/Scenic) and the Black Hills (Hill City, Custer, Keystone, Rapid City, Spearfish, Deadwood, Hot Springs, Sturgis); this is not licence to lift the gate in an ordinary town. Note that 354 private entries database-wide already carried `$$$`/`$$$$` before this, so the gate was never uniformly applied in the first place. Since 2026-10-14 the general form is the published-rate test above (`gateways.py`); the named Badlands and Black Hills towns keep this exception either way.
  - **A rally or festival campground is the event-only case.** Sturgis is the worked example: Glencoe Camp Resort says it is "open to individual camping during the Sturgis Rally only" (14 days a year) and Iron Horse prices every site "for the entire Rally", so both are excluded exactly like a fairground that only camps during events — while Black Hills Vista ("operates seasonally from May 15 through October 1"), Days End, Sturgis RV Park and Rush No More are ordinary seasonal campgrounds that happen to fill for the rally, and go in. Read the operator's own season and rate pages; the site count tells you nothing (Glencoe has 1,000 sites and 4 reviews).

**Local campgrounds:** detect via RV Life `park_type` in {county, city, regional} PLUS a name-scan of the commercial/state buckets for government keywords (county / township / municipal / regional / borough) to catch mislabels (RV Life mis-tags some county/authority parks as commercial). `park_type == dnr` is a state DNR area (state, not local). Do NOT apply the ≥4★/≤$$ gate to local — public government campgrounds are inherently cheap and lightly reviewed; include any confirmed local-government drive-in RV campground and just record the RV Life star/price in the note. Confirm the operator is genuinely local government.

Mechanics:
- **id:** `max(existing id) + 1`; ids are permanent and never reused.
- **Append, don't re-dump:** append entries preserving the file's 2-space indentation; never rewrite the whole file (a full `json.dump` reorders keys / reformats floats / re-escapes unicode and churns the diff). Validate with `json.load` after each batch. To remove an entry, excise just its `{ }` block by id (no braces appear inside values).
- **Fields:** `id`, `kind: "campground"`, `name`, `location` ("lat,lng"), `elevation_meters`, `state`, `ownership`, `waterfront`, `waterfront_evidence` (the waterfront audit proof string; present iff waterfront-audited — see the Waterfront "Record the proof" bullet), `inclusion_evidence` (the validity-audit proof string; present iff inclusion-audited — see the "Inclusion (validity) audit" paragraph), `website`, `phone`, `note`.
- **Coordinates:** pin `location` to the actual campground, not the park HQ/entrance — verify via allstays / recreation.gov / reserveamerica / campsitephotos / official campground-map PDFs (RV Life `_geoloc` is often off by several km).
- **Elevation:** Open-Meteo DEM — `https://api.open-meteo.com/v1/elevation?latitude=<lat>&longitude=<lng>` (comma-separated lists for batch); store meters in `elevation_meters`.
- **`ownership: wma` means parking-lot camping only** (AWH 2026-10-01): a wildlife-management area that allows camping in parking/access lots with no dedicated campsites. A WMA with a genuine campground or marked/designated dispersed sites is `state` (or `local` when a county runs it). The map hides `wma` by default.
- **`waterfront` vocabulary:** on-water `lakefront`/`riverfront`/`creekside`/`pond`/`bayfront` (+ `coastal dunes`/`coastal woods`); view-only `lakeview`/`riverview`/`bayview`; else `not waterfront`. Any non-`not waterfront` value must clear the **evidence gate** in the inclusion-criteria "Waterfront" bullet above (default-down, forbidden sources, mandatory satellite look, recorded `waterfront_evidence`) — never assign from name/marketing/reviews alone.
  - **`riverfront` vs `creekside` is decided by SIZE, not by name** (AWH 2026-10-01). A stream wide enough to read as a river on satellite at the sites — roughly **30 m or more** of open water — is `riverfront` whatever it is called; French Creek PA (~50 m at Utica, ~117 mi long) and Pine Creek PA are rivers. A narrower stream is `creekside`. Measure at the campground, not the stream's widest reach.
  - **`coastal woods` does NOT count as waterfront for the map's "On the water" quick filter** (AWH 2026-07-25). That filter's `ON_WATER` set in `campground_map.html` is `bayfront`/`lakefront`/`riverfront`/`creekside`/`pond`/`coastal dunes` — sites genuinely at the water's edge. `coastal woods` is a set-back-in-the-trees designation with no sightline, so it's excluded there even though the curation rules still treat it as an earned on-water *designation* (i.e. it's a real category with its own evidence bar; it just isn't "on the water" for filtering). Don't "fix" the asymmetry by adding it to `ON_WATER`.
- **Confirm the campground & operator:** reservation directories also list day-use, cabin-only, group, and boat-in/hike-in facilities — verify a drive-in RV campground before adding. Many reservoirs have a USACE dam but state- or USFS-run campgrounds — credit the actual operator, not the dam owner. Don't fabricate phone numbers — verify or omit. **Phone numbers (AWH 2026-10-14, #6):** prefer the number the park publishes (its own site first, then its booking listing) to ours whenever the two differ or ours can't be confirmed on any website.
- **Keep the name, ownership and links current** (AWH 2026-10-14, #8). Rename an entry to the name the park publishes now (a new owner or a rebrand: Joseph Stewart County Park 10641). When the ownership looks wrong, check it and set it from what is actively published (Black River 5088 hipcamp → private; Campus RV Park 1981 local → private). Remove dead or unclaimed Hipcamp links (a 'Coming soon' or ASLEEP listing that takes no bookings), and remove an unclaimed RoverPass link whenever the entry is touched for any reason; a town or county park with no page of its own keeps its aggregator link (above).
- **Note attribution and cleanup:** the `note` is a shared record. When you are already reading or editing a note (never as a pass of its own), rewrite it as one clean, current description instead of appending another snippet: merge duplicates, drop superseded or contradicted facts, and keep dated caveats as plain sentences (AWH 2026-10-13). Text tagged with someone's initials (e.g. `--AWH`) stays in their words with their marker; sign your rewrite once with your own marker (`--Claude`) rather than folding your edits into theirs.
- **URLs belong in `website`, not `note`.** The reservation/official site (recreation.gov, reserveamerica, a state/agency portal, the campground's own domain) goes in the `website` field — newline-separated if several, deduped by domain. Keep website URLs out of `note` prose: write "Reservable online" rather than "Reservable via recreation.gov". Two exceptions stay as prose: a *negative* reservation fact ("FCFS — not on recreation.gov", "book via Campspot, not recreation.gov") and a dead/suspect official domain recorded as a caveat. Source citations (a blog/listicle/video the recommendation came from, e.g. a `campingprepper.com` page) are attributions, not the campground's site — don't move them into `website`; de-URL to a bare name if practical.
- **Prefer deep, campground-specific URLs** over generic portal homepages, and ideally carry BOTH the official park page AND a direct deep reservation link (so booking is one click, no hunting). Deep formats: recreation.gov → `/camping/campgrounds/<id>`; ReserveAmerica → `.../campgroundDetails.do?contractCode=XX&parkId=<id>` or `/explore/<park>/<ST>/<id>/<unit>/campsite-booking`; **US eDirect portals (ReserveOhio, ReserveFlorida, ReserveCalifornia)** → `<frontend>/#!park/<PlaceId>` where PlaceId comes from the citypark API: ReserveOhio `https://ohiordr.usedirect.com/Ohiordr/rdr/fd/citypark`, ReserveFlorida `https://floridardr.usedirect.com/FloridaRDR/rdr/fd/citypark` (each record has `Name`/`Latitude`/`Longitude`/`PlaceId`; match by name+geo). Frontends: ReserveOhio `https://reserveohio.com/OhioCampWeb/#!park/<id>`, ReserveFlorida `https://reserve.floridastateparks.org/Web/#!park/<id>`. Note: `camp.in.gov` redirects to ReserveAmerica (`indianastateparks.reserveamerica.com`), and `goingtocamp` (WI) is Aspira — neither has the clean US eDirect API. **Illinois (ExploreMoreIL)** migrated to a Tyler backend and its OLD `camp.exploremoreil.com/location/<id>` deep links are DEAD (every path 302-redirects to the homepage — don't use them, and fix any you find). Current working format: `https://recreation.exploremoreil.com/IllinoisWeb/Default.aspx#!park/<PlaceId>` (hash route, no redirect). The citypark API is `https://il-rdr.recreation-management.tylerapp.com/IllinoisRDR/rdr/fd/citypark` (`EntityType=="Park"` records carry `Name`/`PlaceId`/geo; its `apiurl` base is inline in the `camp.exploremoreil.com/` HTML). **Verify per-park, not just via the API:** the API lists a park if ANYTHING there is reservable (camping/cabin/shelter), and its place-info URLs have collisions — confirm by fetching the park's own DNR page (`dnr.illinois.gov/parks/camp/park.<slug>.html` or the `dnrhistoric.illinois.gov` site page) and reading the real `#!park/<id>` it links. A DNR camp page that shows only the boilerplate `#!park/3` (Illinois Beach, present on every page) means that park's camping is FCFS (e.g. Pyramid, Cave-in-Rock, Big River SF, the Illinois-River-backwater SFWAs). See [[reference_usedirect_deep_reservation_links]].
