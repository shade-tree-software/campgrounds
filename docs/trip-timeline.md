# Trip detail page: timeline, map, stops and times

Moved out of CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-25 to keep that file small. Treat this as part of the project instructions: read it before touching the code it describes.

Covers `templates/trip_detail.html`, `static/trip-detail/`, and the timeline / route / stop helpers in `ekko_trips_app.py`. GPS track mechanics (tid selection, ping overrides, the overview route overlay) are in `docs/gps-track.md`; the drafted day write-ups are in `docs/rollups.md`.

## Event time zones (from Data Model)

- **Events carry an IANA `tz`, and event ordering is by true instant, not by wall clock.** A trip that crosses a time-zone line breaks naive `"HH:MM"` sorting: driving WEST sets the clock back an hour, so a stop that happened later wears an earlier stamp. Trip 95 hit this on 2026-08-23 — the Macklin Bay overlook (08:47 CDT) really precedes the Benkelman gas stop (08:35 MDT) by 44 minutes, and the timeline showed them backwards. Eastward crossings hide it (the clock jumps forward, which happens to preserve order), which is why it went unnoticed for 95 trips.
  - **The zone is stored, not a UTC timestamp.** `time` stays user-editable, so a stored instant would go stale the moment an admin corrected a wall clock; the zone stays valid. It is also what lets the UI label a time "MDT".
  - **The app layer stamps it, the data layer only reads it.** `_stamp_event_timezone()` in `ekko_trips_app.py` derives `tz` from the event's `location` via `_tz_for_coord` (timezonefinder) on add/update/accept-stops, and detect-stops additionally carries the ping's own zone through in its payload. `trips.py` stays free of that dependency and uses stdlib `zoneinfo` only.
  - **One ranking function, four consumers.** `trips.event_time_rank()` returns minutes-from-UTC-midnight; `_sort_events()` (storage order, which photo directories are keyed on), `_make_trip()`'s timeline sort (display order), `_trip_route_stops()` (the overview map's anchor walk) and the detail map's `byEventTime` (fed the server-computed `time_rank`) all go through it, so no two surfaces can disagree about what happened first.
  - **It is a deliberate no-op where no zones are stamped.** Items with no `tz` are read in the trip's *reference zone* (`reference_timezone()` — the first zone the trip mentions, `""` if none), so every item takes the same constant shift and the order is identical to the old naive sort. Verified: after backfilling all 1,138 events, exactly one trip's timeline order changed — trip 95, at exactly the reported pair — and all 95 precomputed map routes came out byte-identical.
  - **Only the WITHIN-DAY order is normalized.** `sort_date` stays the stored local date: a timeline day is a *local* day ("what we did on the 23rd"), so an offset must never migrate an event onto a neighbouring card group.
  - **`backfill_event_timezones.py`** stamped `tz` on the events written before the field existed (done: all 1,138). It deliberately never re-sorts the stored `events` array — that index keys the photo directories; the next edit's `_remap_indices_after_sort` re-sorts safely.
  - **Times are labelled with their zone only on a trip that spans more than one** (`trip.multi_timezone`, published to JS as `MULTI_TZ`). Keyed on the ABBREVIATION, not the IANA name — `America/Denver` and `America/Boise` both print "MDT", and stamping every time with the same three letters tells the reader nothing. Labelled surfaces: the timeline's time column, the trip-detail map popups and occurrence lists, the overview map's event popups, the detect-stops review rows, and the two home cards (whose times are in the home zone by convention, so `home_tz_abbr` is passed from the route rather than read off an event).
  - **Gap-fill anchors are timestamped in the zone they happened in, and this is load-bearing.** The anchor walk (`_trip_route_stops` server-side, `routeStops` in `static/trip-detail/map.js`) stamps each stay/event/home anchor with a real instant, and that instant decides **which GPS gap the anchor is spliced into** — so a zone error here doesn't nudge the line, it teleports an anchor into a different gap. Both used to stamp everything in one zone (the home zone server-side; the *browser's* zone client-side), which put every Mountain-time anchor on trip 95 two hours early and dropped each morning's first stops into the **previous night's** gap: the line ran from Sunset Point out to Roaring Fork and straight back, a 9.3 km spike, while the travelers were asleep. Six such detours existed across trips 32 and 95 — 3.0/7.5/9.3/8.7/6.1 km on 95 and 5.4 km on 32 — and the client-side half was viewer-dependent, visible from Eastern and invisible from Colorado. Now: an event's zone comes from its `tz`; a stay's and home's from their coordinates (`_tz_for_coord`, memoized per coord in `_trip_route_stops`, published to the client as `tz_offset_min` on each stay/event plus `home_tz_offset_min`). `localEpoch(dateStr, timeStr, offsetMin)` takes the offset as an optional third argument and falls back to browser-local when it's absent, so an item with no resolvable zone behaves exactly as before. **Always pass an offset when adding a new anchor.** `computeTripWindow` passes `HOME_TZ_OFFSET_MIN` because `home_start_time`/`home_end_time` are home-zone by definition — that also makes the client's window agree with `_trip_window_cuts` for a viewer outside the home zone, where the two used to disagree.

## Driving figures: stats page and per-day chip

The stats page's Longest Driving Days and the trip page's per-day driving chip are computed from the same data and must agree; both are specified here.

- `templates/trips_stats.html` — All-time aggregates. Most of the page comes from `_stats_cache` (keyed on the mtimes of trips/campgrounds/home JSON); the photo count and **Longest Driving Days** are deliberately computed outside it, because neither answers to that key — photos are files on disk and a driving day changes when a GPS track is re-fetched. The **Records** section's "Most miles driven" names the trip that covered the most ground (`_most_driven_trip`), summing each day's WHOLE driving (`day_totals`) over the days its trip page shows — so it equals the total of that trip's own day dividers, and a week of day trips out of one campground counts as the real mileage it was rather than the zero that summing A-to-B legs would give. It is **ungated**, unlike the drives below it: a headline fact about a trip rather than a working record, and free per viewer. Both it and the drives are computed outside `_stats_cache` (a re-fetched GPS track changes none of that key's three mtimes) and share one `_drive_stats_inputs()` call — each costs ~24 ms and a contributor's page renders both, so preparing once took that page from 69 ms to 43 ms.

**Longest Driving Days** ranks single-day drives across the whole library (`_longest_drives()`), one row per trip-day rather than one per trip, since a road trip that strung several long days together earned each of them. **It is contributor-only** — admins and `can_upload` users, via `_is_contributor()` (`can_upload` alone is the whole test; the User model turns it on for admins implicitly, and share-link guests are built without it). `_stats_drives()` gates the DATA rather than the markup, so it never reaches a viewer's HTML and the work is skipped for them; that per-viewer answer is also why it can't live in `_stats_cache`, which is shared across viewers. Both numbers cover the span from **leaving where the day started to reaching where it ended**: `_drive_days()` takes the last ping still within `DRIVE_ANCHOR_RADIUS_M` (1 km, campground-sized) of the day's first position and the first ping already within that radius of its last. Bounding by position rather than by mileage is load-bearing — an earlier version trimmed idle ends by cumulative distance and an evening of wander/jitter at Hunting Island (4.5 miles without going anywhere) kept the drive "running" until 23:00, reporting an 8-hour day as 14h40m. Taking the extremes rather than walking contiguous runs is equally deliberate: an errand that leaves and re-enters the circle would stop a run-based scan at the errand. A day that never leaves its starting circle is not a drive and is dropped. Distance is the GPS path length across that span (not the straight line), and **each row carries two durations because they answer different questions**: *total* is elapsed across the span, stops and all — what the day cost you — while *driving* sums only the legs averaging at least `DRIVE_MOVING_MIN_MPS` (5 mph: above walking, below any moving vehicle). Neither alone is honest — a 400-mile day really does take ten hours, but eight at the wheel and two at rest areas is a different day from ten straight through. Moving time is resolution-limited by the ~5 min ping interval; the library-wide check is that moving averages land at 44–60 mph. Days bucket by each ping's own timezone via `_local_date_of_ping` (substituting the home zone for the UTC fallback, which would otherwise push evening driving onto the next day). A trip whose track fails `_track_covers_trip()` contributes no days at all, the same gate that denies it a line on the map.
- **Per-day driving (`_trip_driving_by_day`).** **The trip page no longer prints it** — each day divider used to carry `N stops · N photos · 186 mi · 3h 40m driving` on its right, removed AWH 2026-10-27; the function stays because `process_rollups.py` feeds it to the day write-ups, so the rules below still govern those figures. It reports what the GPS says that day drove — the numbers are precomputed, so unlike the stats page's Longest Driving Days there is no per-viewer work that gating would skip. **A day reports one of two shapes, and the split is what keeps the two pages from contradicting each other:**
  - **A → B** (the day ended somewhere else) → `520 mi · 9h 01m driving`. The figure and the driving time are the LEG, byte-for-byte what the stats page shows for that day. Verified: all 242 shared days match exactly. `local_miles` (anything driven beyond the leg — an evening run to the camp store) is still computed and named in the tooltip, but **deliberately not printed**: it is a couple of miles on a day of several hundred, and on the line it cost a third of the row's width and forced every phone divider to wrap. Don't put it back.
  - **A → A** (`round_trip`) → `81 mi round trip · 2h 31m driving`. There is no leg — `_drive_days` drops the day entirely — so this is the whole day's driving, and the label says which. 64 such days across the library, invisible on the stats page by design.
  - **Reporting the whole day in both places was tried first and is wrong.** It looks equivalent because a big A→B day's end-wander is a rounding error (the visible top-10 rows agreed to within 1.3%), but across all shared days only 14% rounded to the same mileage, the p90 gap was 19 mi, and trip 26's 2024-04-08 read 5 mi on the stats page against 160 mi on the timeline. Don't re-unify them: ranking by whole-day would put a lap around a lake above a cross-country haul, and showing the leg on an A→A day would report 5 mi for a day that drove 160.
  - Both shapes come off one `trip_routes.json` entry (`days` = legs, `day_totals` = whole days) and one pass over the track. A day under `DRIVE_DAY_MIN_M` is omitted by `_trip_driving_by_day` rather than shown as "0 mi", which a reader would take as a measured zero instead of "you didn't go anywhere".

## Trip Detail Layout

Two-column grid (map left, cards right) when `trip.timeline` is non-empty; single-column when truly empty. The map column uses `position: fixed` (not sticky — sticky jitters during scroll); `syncMapColumnPosition()` mirrors the placeholder's `left`/`width` onto the fixed wrapper on load/resize/header-height-change but **never on scroll**. A `.trip-header`-measuring IIFE publishes `--trip-header-height` so the fixed map can anchor below it. Below the 900 px breakpoint the grid collapses to one column and `syncMapColumnPosition()` clears its inline writes so CSS resets aren't fought.

## Trip Detail Map ↔ Card Interaction

**Two-column only.** Clicking a marker scrolls the cards column to the corresponding card via `scrollToCard(cardId)`, which subtracts `--site-top-height` and `--trip-header-height` plus a 16 px gap from the card's `getBoundingClientRect()` Y. Card IDs: `stay-{idx}`, `event-{idx}`, `#home-card-start` (home start). A single-visit family marker picks the sorted visit whose card actually rendered. The reverse (clicking a card `setView`s the map on its marker and pulses it) is also two-column only.

**A family house standing over its own driveway answers for the campspots parked there.** A stay at a `kind: "family"` entry is pinned to its `driveway_location` so it doesn't hide under the red house icon, which leaves the house and the campspot marker ~19–25 m apart — one place to the reader, but historically only the driveway half responded to a click while the house was a proximity marker with no card. `wireStayGroupClick()` now gives both the same behavior (one stay → scroll to its card / details popup; several → the occurrence list), matched by `campground_id` == the family entry's `id` via `campgroundStayKey()`. 24 trips do this, all at the Svendsens' or one other driveway. A family house with **no** stays is unchanged: still just a name, since there's no card to go to.

**A repeated location is the exception on both layouts: it always opens a popup listing every occurrence, and the rows are the picker.** Scrolling somewhere is only a valid answer when there's one place to go. Campspots group by **campground identity** (`stayGroupKey`: `campground_id`, else free-text name + coordinate) rather than by coordinate, because a per-stay `campsite_location` puts two stays at one campground a few metres apart — different coordinates, one marker's worth of pixels (measured across the real data: repeats are 0–50 m apart, sub-pixel at trip zoom, so the pins stack and only the last one drawn was clickable). The markers are deliberately NOT merged — merging would move a pin off its own campsite and cost the per-stay badge number — so instead each pin of a stack opens the same list. Rows lead with the stay's number so they match the map badge and the card circle. `occurrenceListHtml()` builds both this and the family-visit list; the family list drops the per-row event name when every row carries the same one, since then the date+time is the only thing distinguishing them.

**Single-column (≤900 px, where the map stacks above the cards) replaces both with popups.** Following focus across a stacked layout is jarring in both directions — a marker tap scrolls the page out from under the map you just tapped, and a card tap moves a map that's off-screen. So on that layout the map answers in place: marker clicks open a trips-map-style popup (name, place, dates/nights or time, site/description, plus a "View details ›" link that scrolls to the card — the one map→card jump the reader actually asked for), and card clicks don't touch the map. `isSingleColumnLayout()` in `static/trip-detail/map.js` is the single breakpoint check, evaluated **per interaction** so rotating the device takes effect without a reload.

Popups are opened as standalone `L.popup(...).openOn(map)` rather than `bindPopup`, deliberately: binding installs its own click handler, which would pop the popup on the two-column layout too. `openMarkerPopup()` also calls `closeTooltip()` first, because Leaflet opens tooltips on click on touch devices and the two would stack. Popup "View details" links are wired on `popupopen` (delegated over `a[data-card]`), keeping the generated HTML free of inline event attributes.

## Trip Detail Timeline Cards

- Stay cards always render with a body. Event cards (regular events, waypoints, family-visits) go `.bare` whenever there's nothing to put in the body — i.e., the card has no photos AND either it's a waypoint/family-visit OR its description is empty. Bare cards render as just the colored circle + header, transparent background, no body. The "Upload Photos" button stays visible; admins also get an Edit button that opens the shared modal. The body still exists in the DOM for admins so the photo-grid can serve as a cross-card drop target — `body.photo-dragging .event-card.bare .event-body` reveals it absolutely-positioned during a drag without shifting layout. **The cross-grid drop handler keeps `.bare` in sync with the photo count on both sides of the move**: when a photo lands on a bare card the handler removes `.bare` (and reveals the previously-hidden "Remove All Photos" button) so the body stays visible after `dragend` clears `photo-dragging`; symmetrically, when the source card is left with no photos it re-applies `.bare` (and re-hides the button) using the same predicate the Jinja template uses on initial render — waypoint/family-visit OR no description. Without that pairing a dragged-out last photo would leave the source body sitting open and empty, and a dropped-in photo would disappear the moment the drag ended.
- **Compact waypoint rows.** A waypoint with no photos, no description and (for an admin) no `needs_vetting` flag renders as ONE LINE — name, place, how long it stayed (its start time is in the time column like every stop's) — at about a third a card's height, via `wp-compact` in `trip_detail.html`. 29 of trip 95's 92 events and 39 of trip 56's 70. This is what replaced folding runs of them into chips (below): **every stop stays visible and in its own slot**, because those stops are what a reader orients by, but fifty of them stop reading as fifty cards. Notes: it keeps `.bare` as well, so `body.photo-dragging` still reveals a drop target on it — and the JS pairing removes `wp-compact` alongside `bare` when a photo lands, re-adding both when the last one leaves, the same trap the `.bare` pairing documents. The dot is a smaller circle centred in a 2rem-wide slot (the folded-chip device), so the timeline axis still runs through every marker. The row drops the date, which the day divider above already gives, and suppresses a `where` that merely repeats the stop's own name ("Point of Rocks · Point of Rocks, MD"). `tests/test_compact_waypoints.py` pins the predicate and both.
- **Folded waypoint runs — DISABLED 2026-09-10 (`WAYPOINT_FOLDING_ENABLED = False`), kept for reference.** AWH: "I know that people don't care about gas stations, but I use those stops to mentally orient myself about the trip, and I don't like them being collapsed." `_collapse_waypoint_runs()` (server) and `toggleWaypointRun` / `revealWaypointRun` / `releaseWaypointCard` (`static/trip-detail/timeline.js`) folded runs of consecutive content-free waypoints into an `N brief stops ›` chip, in place (never hoisted into the divider) and never across a day boundary; the hidden cards stayed in the DOM so `scrollToCard()`, drag targets and a photo dropped on one kept working. `tests/test_waypoint_runs.py` forces it on so those rules stay pinned. Compact rows (above) are what replaced it; don't re-enable folding without asking.
- **The stay header line reads `<locale, ST> — <nights> (<start> to <end>) · Site <n>`.** Nights are numbered across the whole **visit**, not the stay record — a card says "Night 2 of 3" meaning the trip's second night at that campground, so a stay split across campsites can't read as two separate stays ("1 night" then "Night 1 of 2"). `_make_trip()` puts `night_from`/`night_to`/`night_total` + `card_start`/`card_end` on every timeline stay item and the template picks one of three phrasings from them (whole visit → "N nights"; one night → "Night a of M"; part → "Nights a–b of M"). **Every card shows a date RANGE**, including a single-night copy, so the format never changes between cards. The campsite sits on that same line (on every copy) from `site_label`, a display string `_make_trip()` derives from `site` — blank for the legacy stays that stashed GPS coordinates in `site` (those still render in the details grid instead), and no "Site" prefix when the value already says it. The details grid is skipped entirely when it would be empty.
- **Split multi-night stays**: when a stay spans multiple nights AND at least one event falls on a *strictly interior* day (`arrival < date < departure`), `_make_trip()` emits one timeline copy per night. Each copy carries `copy_num`/`copy_count`. Photo bucketing assigns each photo to the closest-by-EXIF-time copy (rep time = `(arrival + copy_num - 1) at 20:00`); photos without EXIF time fall into copy 1. Copy 1 keeps DOM id `stay-{idx}`; copies 2+ use `stay-{idx}-{N}`. Inline edit form, stay details, campers, notes, and "Remove All Photos" render only on copy 1. Photo grids carry `data-stay-idx="{idx}"` on every copy so `saveGridOrder()` can concatenate filenames across all grids belonging to one stay.
- **Home cards**: `.event-card.bare.home-card` renders at the start and end of the timeline. Their time sits in the time column (below), in a `#home-{start,end}-time-display` span that shows the manual `home_start_time`/`home_end_time` (server-rendered) when set, else the GPS-derived time written client-side by `updateHomeTimeDisplay` in `map.js` — in the same clock/AM-PM markup as the `tl_time` macro, plus a small "auto" line for admins. The auto time is the `home_auto_start_tst`/`home_auto_end_tst` the `/track` response carries (computed by the Python `_find_home_boundary_tsts`); the client only formats and displays it, it doesn't recompute it. Admin Edit prompts are framed as overrides ("GPS-derived time is X; set a manual override only if that looks wrong"). They have no meta line: it only ever carried the date and time, which now live in the divider and the time column.
- **Vertical timeline axis**: all timeline circles are 2 rem × 2 rem inside a header with `padding: 1rem 1.25rem`, so the circle center is always 2.25 rem into the card column. That column now starts `--tl-gutter` (4.6 rem; 3.1 rem ≤ 700 px) in from `.cards-column`'s edge, which is where the time column lives — so the `.cards-column::before` axis sits at `--tl-gutter + 2.25rem`, and nothing else in the column had to change. Cards (`z-index: 1`) hide the line behind their backgrounds, circles (`z-index: 2`) paint above. Bare cards are transparent so the line shows through.
- **The time column** (AWH 2026-09-30: times down the left edge are what make it read as a timeline). Each stop's START time sits left of the axis, level with its circle. The `tl_time` macro emits a zero-height `.tl-time` block directly BEFORE its card and hangs the time out into the gutter with absolute positioning — a sibling rather than a child because every full card has `overflow: hidden` (rounded corners), which would clip anything outside its box; being in flow right above the card is what keeps it pinned there at any card height. `.tl-time.compact` rides higher to match a compact row's smaller dot. On a phone the AM/PM stacks under the clock to keep the column narrow. Events, road legs (the first photo's clock, and only when some photo actually carries one — otherwise `time` is the sort's noon stand-in) and the two home cards get one. **A campspot's is when they got back to it for the night, read off the GPS**, and is blank when there is no trustworthy reading — never a made-up time (see the next bullet). The zone label on multi-timezone trips moved into the column with the time.
- **Campspot comings and goings** (AWH 2026-09-30). The campspot card stays the **last card of its day** and carries the night's return time; every other visit becomes a compact row with the campspot's navy dot, in its own chronological slot: **Arrived at X** (the first arrival, when they went straight back out — trip 92 reached the Svendsens at 5:53 PM, went to the Conshohocken fireworks and came back), **Back at X** (each later return, with how long they stayed), and **Departed X** (the final departure, on the day the stay ends). `_campspot_visits` finds the visits with the same `_visit_windows_at` the "⏱ From GPS" button uses, per VISIT (`visit_runs`, unioning a site move's two records — asking each record reported every return twice on trip 96), from the same kept pings as the day mileage, cached as `camp_visits` in the derived route cache; `_add_campspot_rows` turns them into rows at render time. Rules, each from a real trip:
  - **A return is shown only when something on the timeline happened while away** (an event, waypoint or road card); otherwise the two visits merge. 72 of the library's 237 absences had nothing in them — 1–3 minute GPS flickers, forty minutes somewhere inside a campground as big as Big Meadows — and would have produced "Back at" rows with nothing before them. An outing timed up to `CAMP_OUTING_LEAD_S` (15 min) before the phone leaves still counts: trip 91's paddle starts 18:25 and the phone leaves at 18:30.
  - **Only the FINAL departure gets a row.** An earlier one starts an outing the next card already describes, and it is where overnight silence bites: trip 96's last ping at camp before a morning kayak is 8:03 PM the previous evening. A morning paddle from camp therefore reads paddle → Back at → Departed, which is what happened (trip 92 on Jul 6, 7 and 10; trip 91 on Jun 28).
  - **A time is printed only when the phone reported within `CAMP_TIME_SILENCE_S` (30 min) on the far side of it.** Otherwise a card or row keeps its place with the time blank (and no duration), and a departure is dropped, since its position would be a guess too — except the departure itself: the stay record says which day they left, so "Departed X" goes on that day untimed, right after the last ping at camp when that was on the last day, else at the top of the day (trip 16's Blackwoods and Loraine; AWH 2026-10-01). Only a track that ends before the last day gets no Departed row; every visit in the library has one today (138 timed, 20 untimed). Trip 92's first night at the Svendsens is blank: the phone was silent for 2h46m before the ping at camp, and the fireworks had ended two hours earlier. Library-wide: 212 of 243 campspot cards get a time.
  - **Known quirk, accepted:** a hike or paddle that passes the campsite reads as a return before the card's own end time (5 of 278; trip 56's walk around Fort DeSoto). The phone really was there.
  - The rows carry `data-map-target`, so clicking one frames its campspot on the map like a card header. `tests/test_campspot_rows.py` pins every rule above on synthetic trips.
  - **Arrived / Back at rows take a typed note** (admin: a hover "+ Note" button, then click the text to edit; `PUT /api/trips/<id>/camp-note`). The rows are rebuilt from the track on every render, so the note lives on the visit's first **stay record** as `camp_notes: {"YYYY-MM-DD#N": text}` — the Nth such row for that campspot that day. On the stay rather than the trip because stays renumber and the stay dict travels with itself. Accepted drift: if a re-fetched track merges or splits a day's returns, a note keyed `#2` can land on a different row that day. Departed rows take no note.
- **"1 hr 40 min", not the end time.** An event's meta line is now just its duration, printed bare with no "Stayed" label (AWH 2026-09-30), from the `stayed` template filter (`_stayed`: two `HH:MM` wall clocks, wrapping past midnight, `""` when either is missing or they're equal so nothing reads as a measured zero). Durations print as **"25 min" / "1 hr 5 min" / "2 hr", not the drive chip's "1h 05m"** — a bare "25m" reads as miles or metres; the Arrived/Back at rows and the ⏱ From GPS toast use the same unit, while the driving figures keep `_hm`'s "m". The date left the meta line on full cards too — the day divider says it. A stop with no end time renders no meta line at all.

## Days on the timeline

- **Day dividers lead with "Day N", the date second** (AWH 2026-09-30). **Plain type, not a pill, flush left**: `.day-divider` pulls out over the time column (`margin-left: calc(-1 * var(--tl-gutter))`) so "Day 3" starts at the column's own edge, set at 1.45rem/700 navy (1.3rem on a phone) — well above the 1.05rem card titles, which is what lets it lead without a fill. The row is opaque (`--offwhite`), so the axis breaks for each day heading. N is the CALENDAR offset from the trip's first day, from `_timeline_days()` in `ekko_trips_app.py` — so a three-night stay with nothing on its middle days jumps Day 1 → Day 4 rather than calling the homeward day "Day 2". Day 1 is the earliest of `trip.start` and any card's `sort_date`. The helper covers exactly the dates the template emits dividers for (every `sort_date`, plus `trip.end` when the loop never reached it) and also counts each day's **stops** (every event kind, plus a campspot on the day they ARRIVED — a split stay's later nights are the same stop) and **photos** (whatever the day's cards show: bucketed per night on a split stay, road photos included). It runs last in the route because road cards are spliced in just before. The stop and photo counts are no longer shown on the page (AWH 2026-10-27); only the numbering is used. `tests/test_timeline_days.py` pins numbering, counts and `_stayed`.
- **There is no day picker, deliberately** (AWH 2026-09-30). An "All days" + `‹ Day 3 of 14 ›` stepper that filtered the column to one day and framed it on the map was built, tested and removed the same day at AWH's request — the timeline reads as one continuous trip. Don't re-add a per-day filter without asking.

## Trip Detail Editing UX

- Admin **Edit** and **Upload Photos** buttons live in `.card-actions` (flex sibling of `.stay-info` / `.event-info` in the card header). Desktop: top-right; mobile: drop to row 2 via grid-area `actions`. **Remove All Photos** sits inside `.photos-section` with `align-self: flex-end`. Stay cards render these buttons on copy 1 only.
- Editing paths:
  - Non-bare cards (with photos) use inline edit: `editStay(idx)` / `editEvent(idx)` reveals the `stay-edit-{idx}` / `event-edit-{idx}` block.
  - Bare cards (waypoints / family-visits without photos) and Add buttons use a shared modal: `openAddModal(kind)` / `openEditModal(kind, idx)` → `_openModal(kind, mode, idx)` → form via `_modalFormHtml()`. The event/waypoint form includes a Waypoint checkbox so admins can flip event ↔ waypoint without going through a card.
  - Home cards are structural — no edit/upload controls (manual override is set via the home-card time prompt).
- Both inline and modal paths hit the same backing API endpoints.

## Stop Detection (Admin)

Admin + desktop-only feature on the trip detail page. The **Detect Stops** button in `.trip-header` scans the trip's GPS track for dwell-time clusters not already represented by a stay/event/family location, classifies each as a waypoint (≤30 min) or event (longer), and returns them as advisory suggestions; the admin's selection is POSTed to `/api/trips/<id>/accept-stops`, which creates each via `add_event` with `needs_vetting: true` forced on server-side. Reverse-geocoding runs client-side (1 req/sec, Nominatim policy) with a progress banner and the "Create selected" button disabled until it finishes; closing the modal aborts the run via `AbortController`.

**On-road auto-uncheck:** `_reverse_geocode` returns `on_road: true` when Nominatim's primary hit is `class=highway` with `type in _ON_ROAD_HIGHWAY_TYPES` (motorway/trunk/primary/secondary/tertiary/residential/unclassified/living_street/road + `_link` variants — excludes `service`, `track`, footways). Detect-stops rows tagged this way render dimmed with an "on road · likely traffic" tag and the checkbox defaults to unchecked; the centroid being in the roadway is almost always a traffic jam / stop light rather than a real stop. The signal is free — read off the existing reverse-geocode response, no extra request. Admin can still opt-in by re-checking the box.

**Per-row mini-map:** each detect-stops row carries a small satellite-imagery preview (Esri World_Imagery, 110×80 px) fit to the cluster's ping polyline with a red centroid marker, so the admin can see at a glance whether the cluster sits in a parking lot, at a pull-off, or on a road. The endpoint returns `display.coords` (per-ping lat/lng list) for the cluster; `_initStopMiniMap` in `trip_detail.html` draws the Leaflet map. Lazy-init via IntersectionObserver — 20+ rows would otherwise spin up 20 maps at once for the ~5–8 visible at a time. Re-init on geocoding re-render (rows whose map was already live get an immediate re-init so the visible map doesn't blink empty); not-yet-initialized rows get re-observed. All instances are `.remove()`-d in `closeDetectStops`.

The full pipeline — cluster radius/duration tunables and their calibration, suppressed/relocated exclusion, trip-window narrowing, at-home/near-home classification, anchor-aware streak selection, manual-override precedence, and the known-location/at-home drops (incl. the trip-61/62/87 rationale) — is documented in code, not duplicated here: see the constant comments (`STOP_*`) and the docstrings/comments on `_detect_stops`, `_load_trip_track_for_detection`, `_find_home_boundary_tsts`, `_drop_stops_at_known_locations`, and `api_detect_stops` in `ekko_trips_app.py`.

**Dismissed stops — a stop you added and then deleted is flagged, not re-offered clean.** Detection re-scans the same track every run. A trip's own stays and events suppress a cluster (`_drop_stops_at_known_locations`), so an accepted stop stops being offered — but *deleting* that event removes exactly the anchor that was suppressing it, so the stop reappears **because** it was rejected, indistinguishable from a fresh find. `trips.delete_event` therefore records a `dismissed_stops` entry (`{lat, lng, date, name, removed_at}`) on the trip record for any deleted event that has coordinates, and `_flag_dismissed_stops` marks matching clusters after the known-location drop (which is where the only clusters left to flag are the ones whose event is gone). Notes:
- **It FLAGS rather than drops**, mirroring the `on_road` treatment: the row still renders, dimmed, unchecked, with a purple "removed before · <date>" tag. The admin's earlier "no" is information, not a veto — they may have deleted the event for an unrelated reason, and a row withheld is one they can never reconsider. Re-checking the box creates it again.
- **Matching is by position AND date**, the same convention (and the same `STOP_NEAR_ANCHOR_M` radius and same any-ping-in-the-cluster test) the stay/event anchors use, for the same reason: the same rest area outbound and on the return is two different stops. A dismissal with no date matches on position alone.
- **Recorded for ANY deleted event with a location, not just detect-stops ones.** There's no durable origin marker on an event (`needs_vetting` is cleared by the first edit), and the rule reads the same either way: if you deleted an event at a place, you don't want detection proposing it.
- **`/accept-stops` retires a dismissal it re-creates** (`clear_dismissed_stops_near`, ~55 m tolerance for centroid drift between runs) — accepting is the admin changing their mind, and a stale dismissal would flag the stop again the moment anything near it was deleted.
- **Prospective only**: events deleted before this existed left no record, so they're still offered as new.
- `_iso_date_or_none` / `_cluster_local_date_range` / `_any_cluster_ping_within` were lifted out of `_drop_stops_at_known_locations` to module level so both matchers share one implementation — keep them shared rather than re-nesting.

`needs_vetting` is a boolean field on events. `true` items render with a dashed amber outline and a "Needs review" badge on the timeline card, plus a dashed amber ring on the map marker — mirroring on every surface so the admin can find them. The flag is cleared by *any* PUT to the event: both the inline and modal save handlers inject `needs_vetting: false` into the payload, so simply opening + saving the edit form is the act of vetting. The field defaults to `false` in `_make_trip()` for older trip records that predate the field, so existing data renders unchanged. Tunables (radius/duration constants) live at module level in `ekko_trips_app.py` near the helpers.

**Trip-boundary calculation has one implementation.** `_find_home_boundary_tsts()` in `ekko_trips_app.py` is the sole home-boundary detector (its docstring carries the full algorithm and the SINGLE-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH contract). It feeds two consumers: (1) `api_trip_track` returns `home_auto_start_tst` / `home_auto_end_tst` in the `/track` payload, which the frontend uses for both the polyline window cuts (`computeTripWindow`) and the home card's "(auto)" time — the frontend never recomputes; (2) detect-stops calls it directly. Manual `trip.home_start_time` / `trip.home_end_time` win over the auto values on both. **Do not reintroduce a JS reimplementation** — a prior one (`findHomeBoundaryTimes`) drifted and was deleted. The constants (`STOP_NEAR_HOME_M`, `STOP_AT_HOME_CENTROID_M`, `STOP_HOME_BOUNDARY_LOCK_S`, `TRACK_NEAR_STAY_KM`) live only on the Python side.

**A departure on a later day than the trip started is withheld, because the track doesn't contain the departure at all.** `home_departure_tst` is the away streak's FIRST ping, which is a home departure only if the track begins at home — nothing in the algorithm checked that, and it isn't true when somebody else drives the EKKO out: their phone isn't reporting, the trip's own `bad_track_windows` correctly carves those days away, and the first surviving ping is hundreds of miles out, days later. Trip 58 left on a Tuesday and its earliest ping is **Friday 12:39 at Royersford, 131 miles from home**, which the home card printed as the Tuesday departure (trip 47 does the same across a Thursday→Saturday gap). Passing `trip_start_date` makes the detector return `None` for a departure on a later local date; the card falls back to blank and an admin sets `home_start_time`, the same manual override trips 37 and 30 already carry. **It is a DATE test and deliberately not a distance one.** The ordinary failure is OwnTracks going quiet over the real departure, leaving the first away ping tens of miles down the road on the RIGHT day — trip 2 after a 213-minute silence (26 mi out), trip 5 with nothing between home at 10:28 and camp at 21:31 (46 mi out). Those times are late but the day is right and the drive is real, and a distance threshold separating them from the driven-out trips would have to sit somewhere between 2.3 and 26 miles with nothing to anchor it; a wrong DAY is unambiguous. The arrival is untouched — it is the first at-home ping after the streak, so it is anchored at home by construction. **Blast radius, measured across all 87 trips with a track:** six boundaries change (43, 46, 47, 51, 58, 64) and **no trip's polyline or mileage moves**. For 47 and 58 the kept-ping set is identical (the window's lower bound was already the first surviving ping). The other four are the phone-stayed-home trips that fail `_track_covers_trip` both before and after — trip 43 is the one `process_rollups.track_is_trustworthy` documents — so their wider window is inert. `tests/test_home_boundary.py` pins the rule, the deliberate non-effect on a same-day gap, and that omitting `trip_start_date` reproduces the old behaviour exactly.

## The Day's Write-Up

One slot under each day divider on the trip page, and **two things can fill it: a note somebody typed, and a rollup drafted by `process_rollups.py`. The typed note always wins** (`_trip_day_writeups`). Measured against trip 95, the drafts were accurate and mostly restated the cards printed directly below them — AWH 2026-09-10: "they basically repeat what anyone can read later on in the timeline for that day" — so the cheap answer got the slot. A person types the part the facts cannot supply; a draft stands in front of nothing it can beat.

- **A note lives on the TRIP RECORD (`day_notes: {"YYYY-MM-DD": text}` in `trips.json`), not in `day_rollups.json`, and that split is the point.** A draft is regenerable on whichever host holds the API key, so its file is gitignored, excluded from `sync-from-pa.sh` and absent on a fresh clone. A sentence someone typed exists nowhere else — in `trips.json` it rides home from the live host by the same path as every other thing written about a trip, gets backed up, and survives a redraft.
- **Editing a draft does not edit the draft** — it writes a note over it. So a generated sentence can never be quietly reworded into something a person appears to have said, and the draft is still there if the note is cleared. The attribution line ("Drafted by <model>") keys on `model`, which a note doesn't carry; that is the same generated-vs-human line the app draws everywhere else.
- Blank text deletes the key, and the whole `day_notes` dict once it empties, so a cleared note leaves no trace and lets any draft show through again. **The endpoint answers with the RESOLVED write-up for that day** (`writeup`, null when the slot is really empty) rather than just the text that was typed, and the client renders whatever comes back — because clearing a note doesn't empty the day, it uncovers the draft the note was standing in front of. It used to return only `text`, so the slot went blank in place and the draft reappeared on the next reload: two different answers to the same question. `_trip_day_writeups` stays the single definition of what belongs in that slot; `dayWriteupHtml()` in `static/trip-detail/timeline.js` mirrors the `day_divider` macro's markup and must stay in step with it.
- **`parse_trips()` does NOT carry `day_notes` — read them with `get_day_notes(trip_id)`.** `_make_trip()` builds a display object and doesn't copy the field, so `trip.get("day_notes")` on a parsed trip is ALWAYS empty and a check written that way reports zero notes however many exist. That is not hypothetical: it produced a confident "you have no day notes anywhere in the library" while seven sat in the file, and nearly led to a PA→local sync that would have deleted four of them.
- Admin-only (`PUT /api/trips/<id>/day-note`), matching every other prose field — stay notes and event descriptions are admin too. Click the text to write over it; a day with nothing gets a "+ note about this day" link. `tests/test_day_notes.py` pins storage and precedence.
- **The day they got HOME needs a divider of its own, and for seven trips it never had one.** A stay's card sits on the night it began, so a trip that drove home the next morning ends its timeline loop on an earlier date — the home end card then landed under the wrong day's divider and the homeward day had no slot at all. `process_rollups.py` had been drafting those days all along (the record names them: a stay's `end`), and the text was rendered nowhere. `trip_detail.html` now emits one more divider after the loop when `trip.end > ns.last_date`, through a **`day_divider()` macro** the loop also calls — extracted precisely so the two emissions can't drift, since the write-up slot is the part that must not. Symmetrically, `trips_days()` in the drafter now unions the record's dates with the timeline's `sort_date`s: the record knows the homeward day, the timeline knows the interior nights of a split multi-night stay (trip 21's 2023-11-24 had a divider and a slot that nothing was ever drafted for). **A day on neither list is a day nobody can see.**
- **A single-day trip has no day dividers** (the timeline only emits them when the trip spans more than one date), so it has nowhere to put one. Its trip note is the equivalent.

## Times from GPS (Admin)

A card added while the trip is happening carries the moment the admin got around to opening the form — a few minutes after actually arriving — and almost never an end time, because by the time you leave you're thinking about the road rather than the form. The **⏱ From GPS** button beside the Time field on every event / waypoint / family-visit edit form (inline and modal both) reads the real arrival and departure off the trip's own track and fills the two time inputs. It **never saves**: the times come off a heuristic and the admin is the one who was there, so cancelling the form is the undo.

`GET /api/trips/<id>/gps-times` is **coordinate-addressed, not event-addressed** (`lat`/`lng`, plus the form's current `date`/`time`/`exclude_event` and an optional `radius_m`), so it answers for what the form SHOWS — a location just moved with Pick on Map, or a card that hasn't been saved yet.

- **`_visit_windows_at` splits the track into the separate visits made to one place, on two radii.** At-place is 300 m (`VISIT_AT_PLACE_M`); only a ping past 600 m (`VISIT_DEPART_M`) ends a visit. The band between them is stationary drift or a walk to the far side of a big site — it neither extends a visit nor ends one. Either radius alone fails: a tight one splits a three-hour museum stop into four (a parked phone's fix wanders the same 100–200 m `STOP_CLUSTER_RADIUS_M` is sized for), a loose one merges the gas station and the diner across the road into one visit spanning the drive between them.
- **Elapsed time is never a break condition.** OwnTracks suspends reporting while a device sits still, so a genuine multi-hour dwell can be an arrival ping, a departure ping and nothing in between — splitting on the gap would cut exactly the visits this exists for in half.
- **Each ping is credited to the nearest card OF THAT DAY.** Without that, trip 95's Hy-Vee (346 m from North Park) reported a 17:02 arrival for an 18:02 stop, swallowing the hour spent at the park; Kaibab Park and the Shell 221 m away merged the same way. Scoping rivals to the same day is what keeps the rule from backfiring on one place visited twice: the trip's two Forest Canyon Overlook cards are pinned 196 m apart (Rainbow Curve 19 m, Kaibab 5 m), and letting those compete collapsed a real 30-minute visit to a single fix. They sit closer together than genuine neighbours do, so distance can't separate them — but they are never about the same moment. `exclude_event` keeps the card being edited from competing with itself; `VISIT_SELF_ANCHOR_M` (60 m) catches a card pinned on top of it.
- **Deliberately not `_detect_stops`.** That answers "where did we linger?" with no knowledge of the card: its clusters form around a running centroid and can settle hundreds of metres off the pin, and its 4-minute floor yields nothing for a 2-minute waypoint that still has a real arrival time.
- Measured over the whole library (1,116 located events): 98.3% match a visit, half agree with the hand-entered time to the minute, 85% within 5 minutes, and 990 gain an end time they didn't have. The misses are honest ones — boat tours, kayaking, a summit hike — where the card names where you *went*, not where the phone sat. The response carries `gap_minutes` so the toast can warn that the matched visit is an hour or more from the time on the card instead of silently rewriting it.
- `tests/test_visit_windows.py` pins the two-radius rule, the silence rule and the rival rule.
