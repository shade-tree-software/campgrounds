SEASON DATES FROM NOTES — first pass (AWH 2026-11-07)

You read campground notes and write down WHEN EACH CAMPGROUND IS OPEN, as two MM-DD dates.
Read only the text you are given. No web, no browser, no other files. Do not edit
campgrounds.json or anything in the repo. Your only output is one JSON file.

INPUT: a JSON list. Each row has id, name, state, ownership, season (what is stored now: it
says the campground is seasonal, `year_round: false`, but has no dates; it may carry a
season `note`), season_source (where that stored season came from), and note (the entry's
prose note).

OUTPUT: a JSON list with exactly one object per input row, in input order, written to the
path you are given. Each object is one of:

  {"id": N, "opens": "MM-DD", "closes": "MM-DD", "exact": true|false,
   "approx_text": "..." or null, "quote": "...", "why": "..."}
  {"id": N, "year_round": true, "quote": "...", "why": "..."}
  {"id": N, "skip": "short reason"}

- quote: the words in the note or season note that you took the dates from, copied VERBATIM
  (a substring someone can search for). Required on every non-skip row.
- why: one short clause on how you turned the wording into dates ("bare months -> whole
  months", "Memorial Day -> 05-25 per table").

WHAT COUNTS
Only the campground's OPERATING season: the nights a person can camp there. NOT:
- the reservation/booking window ("reservable May 15 - Sep 30", "reservations taken
  April-October"). A reservation season is not a campground season. If the note says
  reservations run X-Y and first-come camping happens outside that, SKIP.
- the season of water, showers, restrooms, flush toilets, the dump station, electric
  service, fees, the store, the beach, boat rentals, a marina, hunting, fishing.
  "Water May-Oct" says nothing about when you can camp. (But "open May-Oct; water
  off in winter" -> May-Oct is the camping season, fine.)
- "peak season", "busiest", "best in fall", "summer rates", "weekly rates in winter".
- a one-time past event ("closed in 2024 for repairs").
When the note is unclear whether the dates are the camping season or something else, SKIP.

WHEN LOOPS / CAMPGROUNDS DIFFER: take the WIDEST span (earliest opening, latest closing) over
the parts that take RVs. If the note says part of it is open year-round (a winter loop, a
plowed area, "Loop A open all year"), return {"year_round": true} with the quote — that is
the answer for "can we camp here", and the loop detail stays in the note.

A season with a hole in the middle (open spring and fall, closed in summer), or two
unrelated ranges, -> SKIP.

Only one end known ("closes after Labor Day", "opens May 1") -> SKIP. Both ends are needed.

Words with no month — "summer", "spring through fall", "warm months", "snow-free months",
"ice-out", "when the road opens", "weather permitting" alone — -> SKIP. "Weather permitting"
next to months is fine: use the months.

DATE RULES (the 2026-10-20 season pass's convention; follow it exactly)
- A stated day: use it. "May 15 to Oct 1" -> 05-15 / 10-01. A tilde still counts ("~Apr 15").
  A year-specific statement ("2026: April 1 - October 12") is used as the recurring season.
- A bare month: the whole month. Opening month -> its 1st; closing month -> its last day.
  "May-October" -> 05-01 / 10-31. "April through mid-October" -> 04-01 / 10-15.
- "mid-<month>" -> the 15th (either end).
- "early <month>": opening -> the 1st; closing -> the 7th.
- "late <month>": opening -> the 20th; closing -> the month's last day.
- Alternatives ("Apr 15/May 1 - Oct 15/31", "April or May") -> the widest: earliest opening,
  latest closing.
- A floating weekday rule ("second Friday in April", "first weekend in May") -> its widest
  possible date: earliest possible for an opening, latest possible for a closing.
- Holidays (widest possible date):
    Memorial Day -> 05-25          Memorial Day weekend (opening) -> 05-22
    Thursday before Memorial Day -> 05-21
    Labor Day / Labor Day weekend (closing) -> 09-07   after Labor Day (opening) -> 09-02
    Independence Day / July 4 -> 07-04      Canada Day -> 07-01
    Columbus Day, Indigenous Peoples' Day, Canadian Thanksgiving (closing) -> 10-14
    US Thanksgiving (closing) -> 11-28      Thanksgiving weekend (closing) -> 12-01
    Victoria Day (opening) -> 05-18         Victoria Day weekend (opening) -> 05-15
    Easter (opening) -> 03-22               Fete nationale / Saint-Jean-Baptiste -> 06-24
  Any other holiday or an event ("opening day of trout season", "deer season") -> SKIP.
- A season running over New Year (a winter-only campground) is fine: opens 11-01, closes
  03-31. Do not swap them.
- Months are leap-free: February ends 02-28.

exact: true ONLY when BOTH ends came from a stated day (including ~ and year-specific).
Anything that used a month, mid/early/late, a holiday, a weekday rule or alternatives ->
false.

approx_text (exact:false only): a short phrase for the reader saying what the source
actually states, written as a sentence fragment after "Approximate: ". Examples:
  "open May to October."   "Memorial Day to Labor Day."   "mid-April to late October."
  "the Thursday before Memorial Day to Labor Day."
Use the note's own wording, tidied, never invented precision. Set it to null only when the
stored season note ALREADY states that same wording (then it is marked already).

CONFLICTS
- The note says open year-round / all year / winter camping, but no loop detail? Return
  {"year_round": true}. The stored `year_round: false` may simply be wrong; quote it.
- The note and the stored season note disagree on the dates -> prefer the SEASON NOTE when
  season_source is a web page or an agency (it was written from that source later), and
  say so in why. If you can't tell which is current, SKIP with "conflict: ...".

CLARIFICATIONS FROM EARLIER CHUNKS
- When the stored season note already states the wording, set approx_text null; don't restate it.
- A road or gate closure stated beside the campground's own season is not the season: use the
  campground's.
- A one-year extension or exception ("in 2026 extended to Nov 22", "this year open through
  October") is NOT the recurring season: use the base season it extends. (A year-specific
  statement of the whole season, "2026: April 1 - October 12", still counts.)
- recreation.gov first-come or fee-season nights may widen a stated season but are never the
  only source, except at a reservation-only campground whose rec.gov nights ARE its season.
- "Closed November to April" style: invert to the widest OPEN reading (reopens at the start of
  the last closed month named: 04-01; closes at the end of the month before: 10-31).
- A fairgrounds closed only for fair week: date the season and ignore the fair week (it only
  makes fair week look open). A longer hole (a whole month) is still a SKIP.
- "Nth weekend": count the weekend from its Saturday.

Be conservative: a skip costs nothing (the campground just stays "unknown" on the map); a
wrong date hides a campground that is open. When in doubt, skip.

When done, report: counts (dated exact / dated approx / year_round / skip), the skip reasons
grouped roughly, and up to 10 rows you were least sure of (id + why).
