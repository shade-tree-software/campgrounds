Lessons from recent batches (IL/VT/WI/SK and the Montana round): booking.fcfs values are always / after_cutoff (reservations close N days or hours before arrival and open sites then go walk-up - set reserve_until too) / some_sites / never; nothing else. fees.extra_person is an object. Some phones are stored as an empty string: give new phones as empty -> new. Check that stored inclusion_evidence, note and links describe THIS campground (a Wisconsin entry carried a Washington park's data; Saskatchewan pins and evidence described a neighbouring private park or cottage subdivision); if not, give replacement text. Whole-park entries' stored evidence often has wrong counts and loop names: measure every campground in the park. Several stored names are wrong or out of date: give the agency's current name. An --AWH waterfront value is one signed --AWH in note or evidence. Season dates: give the 2027 date where it differs (2027 dates are rarely out in November: write 2026 and say so); a reservation window is not a season; a pool's or rink's season on a town page is not the campground's. Pins often sit at an office, entrance, gate, trailhead or day-use lot, and sometimes on the wrong lake: place the camping loops. For any private, town or county park that looks seasonal, measure the nightly share and give the Google rating + count; when kept at 10-50% nightly, the note says so plainly ("Mostly seasonal: about N of its M sites are sold by the night"). A park whose only posted rate is seasonal or monthly fails test 2; under 1 site in 10 nightly fails test 4; a park that sells or leases lots fails test 5 (a resort that also sells titled RV lots: quote the lot wording and measure the rental campground separately); a private park with no price anywhere and only a Facebook/directory page is a drop candidate; a mostly-seasonal private park needs a posted nightly rate or a booking site; but 'no posted nightly rate' is not a drop for an ordinary private park with presence and an RV Life $/$$ tier (Montana lesson: two parks were wrongly dropped on it). Price gate: the test is the cheapest real RV night (an electric site a 23-ft rig can book) in ANY season - a park whose spring/fall or weekday RV nights are $50 or under passes even if summer weekends are higher (AWH rulings 2026-10-30, 2026-11-16). No-hookup or tent-only sites don't count as the RV night. Report the summer peak too; a park that passes only narrowly and has mediocre Google reviews (under ~4.5) is a drop candidate for the reviewer, not an automatic drop. A booking engine's terms checkbox is not to be ticked: report the nightly share as unmeasured. Casino RV parking along a casino lot or truck-stop lot is not a campground. A fair association's campground stays local. A general campground with ATV trail access stays (just say so); an off-road park or OHV staging camp does not. Horse rule (any owner): horse sites mixed into the general loops with no separate horse area are a drop candidate; a mere amenity line or a single sold horse site in a general campground is not; a horse camp beside a general campground with its own loop is not the entry. A source that refuses bots is not dead: keep its link unless it is actually gone. A regional or town park can be long closed while directories still list it: check news and the agency's own list. When a stored count, rig length or amperage has no source, say so and give the sourced value if you found one. Never copy personal names (hosts, owners) from booking-site or review text. From the Montana round (federal-heavy, the nearest precedent): a forest/BLM campground with no recreation.gov listing is first-come only; "motorhomes not recommended", "trailers not recommended", "very tight", "limited RV access" or a high-clearance recommendation on the access road were drops; a "steep access road" with no rig restriction was kept, and an agency-named camper route (from one direction) with no clearance warning was kept with the route in the note; a day-use site where camping means the parking lot is not a campground; walk-in-only sites are not drive-in; closure orders and group-only campgrounds are out; a public campground with no agency page was kept on a Google Maps listing with a phone (call-first note) or a recent review confirming camping. Sources that refuse (don't retry): koa.com (Cloudflare; Campspot may also 403 - use the earlier captures under audit/price_research/), www.campspot.com (403 from SK), ResNexus (Incapsula - never open it in the browser), checkfront.com (Cloudflare), Firefly availability (reCAPTCHA), reservation.pc.gc.ca, Overpass (406 - don't use mirrors), Nominatim (access denied). nws.usace.army.mil 403'd from the cloud in the Montana round. When an operator's own site refuses, still look for it on Campspot, RoverPass, CampLife, Facebook or Google, and say what the earlier captures under audit/price_research/ show. A Google rating shared with a bar, motel, golf course or attraction is not the campground's alone - say so. Never run git, even read-only. Stored potable-water, electric and site-count values from the sweep notes are often wrong - check each against the FS page.

From p3-553: a campground absent from the forest's full camping list, RIDB and imagery is closed (Haskin Creek) - check imagery before calling it. FS pages often contradict rec.gov on reservable sites - write rec.gov and flag. Refusing: go-wyoming.com (403), glenrock.org (406).

From p3-562: a city's old domain may be hijacked (highmore.org now a law-firm spam page) - never cite a page that doesn't name the campground. A city with no web page at all: keep on the Google listing + dated camper reports, note says call-first. A motel/lodge RV park whose own site never mentions RV sites may fail the presence test - say so in flags.
From p3-563: Facebook-only private park with no rate anywhere = drop on price+presence (5842). Gateway rule (gateways.py) can pass a private park over the price gate (Rafter J $94.95, Mount Rushmore 11 km). GFP parks: check the website's facID matches THIS park (Palisades carried Sandy Shore's 57).
From p3-564: an old waterfront evidence line may describe TENT-ONLY sites (Angostura 15/16) - re-measure from the nearest RV site. A private park selling RV spots only by the month = drop (T-Bone's).
From p3-566: a stale curation_note ('Priced above the sweep's usual private gate') should be replaced with the dated gate result. Campspot JSON needs the in-page x-booking-token header. An old lakefront evidence line often describes a tent/day-use tip - re-measure from the RV pads.
From p3-567: a TLS certificate mismatch is a bot wall too - read Wayback; never curl -k. A tailrace campground sits below the dam - name the river, not the reservoir above. Mandatory per-person admission on a private attraction counts toward the price gate (ask when it is conditional).
From p3-569: a booking value derived from note prose ('first-come always') is the weakest kind - an engine found on the operator page replaces it. A military promo code is not a membership discount for the price gate.
From p3-570: a stale 'priced above the gate' curation_note gets replaced with the dated gate result whenever the batch reads a price. Marketing 'along the banks of' a river is checked against imagery and the note corrected.
From p3-571: check that a pin isn't sitting on a NEIGHBOURING campground (Echo Valley's pin was on Heritage Village's loops). Derived fields with no source on the operator page may have been borrowed from a same-town entry - flag them.
From p3-573: evidence measured at a wrong pin is no evidence - re-read waterfront whenever a pin moves far. A single-page-app site with an empty HTML shell may carry its rates in the JS bundle.
From p3-574: an age-restricted park is kept with the cutoff leading the note ('AGE-RESTRICTED: 40 and older'), per the FL precedent - not a drop. A 'Book Now' button that opens a contact form is phone booking, not online.
From p3-576: a fee tier copied from a same-town park (Clear Lake's $30 FHU was Ulven Park's) is a common derived error - check the tier against this park's own page.
From p3-578: a city page can list a lake campground it says it does not run (Menno Lake) - read the operator line before keeping 'local'; association-run = private.
From p3-579: a site sold only behind Incapsula (ResNexus) is unreadable in Wayback too - keep the last dated read and say so.
From p3-572: an open UNUSED sand or grass apron counts as on-water within ~50 m (curation doc's apron rule); a used beach in between (the campground's own swim beach, day-use lawn) is a buffer - measure to the high-water edge past it only when it is unused.
From AWH 2026-11-23 (curation doc, bluff/bench clause): on the water only if a camper can easily reach the water and launch a kayak - pads 1-2 m above the edge over a gentle bank (to ~40%) pass; a 2-3.7 m bank at ~30 deg is a view. Creekside is the exception: an easy walk down to the creek is enough (Caledonia PA 655: 2.2-2.75 m over 21-28%). A booking-site field that contradicts the site's own photos and its other fields (Caledonia 133's '7 ft overhead clearance' beside 'max vehicle 50 ft') is an error, not a limit - report it, don't drop on it.
From p3-581 (NH): a booking site's 'Riverfront'/'Lakefront' tag on a site a 23-ft rig can't use (Tent Only, Pop-up 16-19 ft) supports nothing - measure from the nearest site the rig fits. Re-read old evidence against where it was taken: Baker River's 'riverside clearing' was read at a pin 330 m off in the woods.
From p3-582 (NH): the 10-50% nightly band keeps a park with a caveat (AWH 2026-11-12) and tent sites count toward the nightly share (AWH 2026-10-30): count transit + tent sites against the whole park before calling a drop. A state or local park is never price-gated - report a high rate, don't propose a drop on it.
From p3-586 (KY): rec.gov's 'Lakefront' site tag is loose on Corps/FS lakes (Holly Bay A-7 ~120 m off) - plot the site points and measure; never take the tag alone. A city RV park whose page is blank may still run a booking engine with current rates (Pikeville: pikevillebooking.com). A private park posting only monthly/seasonal/yearly-contract rates is a drop (Cedars RV Park), even if an older note called it transient.

An operator page's '$45 & up' can be stale against its own engine ($55 every date) - the engine quote wins. Dale Hollow's horse campers have their own pods (P-R): horse areas explicitly separate = keep, leave those pods out of the fees.
From p3-588 (KY): parks.ky.gov/explore/<slug>-NNNN links now 404 for some parks - the live page is parks.ky.gov/<town>/parks/<type>/<slug>; give the new one. parks.ky.gov/improvements lists campground closures that ReserveAmerica does not reflect - read it for every state park.
