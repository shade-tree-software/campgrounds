Lessons from recent batches (IL/VT/WI, and the Alberta round): booking.fcfs values are always / after_cutoff (reservations close N days or hours before arrival and open sites then go walk-up - set reserve_until too) / some_sites / never; nothing else. fees.extra_person is an object. Some phones are stored as an empty string: give new phones as empty -> new. Check that stored inclusion_evidence, note and links describe THIS campground (a Wisconsin entry carried a Washington park's data); if not, give replacement text. An --AWH waterfront value is one signed --AWH in note or evidence. Season dates: give the 2027 date where it differs (2027 dates are rarely out in November: write 2026 and say so); a reservation window is not a season. Pins often sit at an office, entrance, gate or day-use lot: place the camping loops. For any private, regional, town or RM park that looks seasonal, measure the nightly share and give the Google rating + count; when kept at 10-50% nightly, the note says so plainly ("Mostly seasonal: about N of its M sites are sold by the night"). A park whose only posted rate is seasonal fails test 2; under 1 site in 10 nightly fails test 4; a park that sells or leases lots fails test 5; a private park with no price anywhere and only a Facebook/directory page is a drop candidate; a mostly-seasonal private park needs a posted nightly rate or a booking site. Price gate: the test is the cheapest real RV night (an electric site a 23-ft rig can book) in ANY season - a park whose shoulder-season or weekday RV nights are C$69 or under passes even if summer weekends are higher (AWH rulings 2026-10-30, 2026-11-16). No-hookup or tent-only sites don't count as the RV night. Report the summer peak too; a park that passes only narrowly and has mediocre Google reviews (under ~4.5) is a drop candidate for the reviewer, not an automatic drop. A fair or exhibition association's campground stays local. A general campground with ATV trail access stays (just say so); an off-road park or OHV staging camp does not. Horse rule (any owner): horse sites mixed into the general loops with no separate horse area are a drop candidate; a mere amenity line or a single sold horse site in a general campground is not. A source that refuses bots is not dead: keep its link unless it is actually gone. When a stored count, rig length or amperage has no source, say so and give the sourced value if you found one. A Google rating shared with a golf course, bar or attraction is not the campground's alone - say so. Saskatchewan regional parks often bundle a golf course, a beach and a seasonal section: the campground's own nightly sites are what count. When an operator's own site refuses, still look for it on Let's Camp, Campspot, RoverPass, Facebook or Google, and say what the earlier captures under audit/sk_private/ and audit/price_research/ show. Saskatchewan sources that refuse (don't retry): reservation.pc.gc.ca (403). Overpass refuses (406): don't use mirrors. Never run git, even read-only. From p3-520: the parks.saskatchewan.ca site-list (paging) table carries type, amps and max length for every site - read it first and fetch campsite detail pages only for key sites (the server slowed to ~30 s a page); stored Sask Parks phones are mostly empty - Tourism Saskatchewan's parks data ('Phone Number Primary') gives the park office; whole-park entries' stored inclusion_evidence often has wrong counts and loop names - give replacement text; pins can sit kilometres off (Candle Lake's was a marina lot 5.8 km away). From p3-521/522: provincial recreation sites leased to an operator (Borden Bridge, Besant, Michel Point, Nesslin Lake) book and charge on their own - say so and recommend policy_ref none if not already set, since the Sask Parks row would print the wrong booking system and entry permit. A whole-park entry's stored pin and evidence often describe a different loop or a neighbouring business (The Battlefords' pin sat on a golf resort's RV loops): measure every loop. A Sask Parks site row 'Near Water - no' on every site is good evidence for not waterfront. From p3-523: there is no local:SK policy row, so a regional park's per-vehicle entry fee goes in fees.entrance as {resident, nonresident, per}; a regional park's shore rows are often the seasonal ones - check that a water value rests on sites Let's Camp sells by the night. Refusing: sasklakes.ca (403). From p3-524: Tourism Saskatchewan map points are often wrong (Yorkton, Fort Qu'Appelle, Eagle Valley) - don't pin from them. Refusing: eaglevalleypark.ca (406), Nominatim (access denied). From p3-525: stored lakefront values on Sask Parks entries keep resting on a park map's shore row; the SKPP GIS points per site plus Esri usually show forest between - measure them. Sask Parks campsite photos (where they show the lake) are what can hold a lakeview. From p3-526: Let's Camp site photos are the best sightline evidence for regional parks - read them for every shore row. Old pins and evidence inside provincial parks can sit on a private campground in the park (Saskatchewan Landing's on Landing View). Refusing: www.campspot.com (403) - campreservations.ca now redirects there. From p3-527: saskregionalparks.ca GPS points can be kilometres off (Wakaw ~3 km) - pin from imagery. A booking engine's terms checkbox (RezExpert) is not to be ticked: report the nightly share as unmeasured. From p3-528: several stored 'Regional Park' names are town/city campgrounds (Melfort, Grenfell) - check saskregionalparks.ca lists the park and give the operator's name. A pool's or rink's season on a town page is not the campground's. From p3-529: a stored 'pond' can be a park swimming pool - check imagery. Village campgrounds with no website: say what Tourism Saskatchewan and the town page show and keep the groups thin rather than filling from directories. saskregionalparks.ca now answers plain curl (no captcha).
