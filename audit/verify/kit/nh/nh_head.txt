Report any entry whose stored ownership breaks this. FEDERAL (WMNF): rec.gov / RIDB campsite catalog for fit, hookups (none expected), fees, season and first-come vs reservable (trust rec.gov over older notes and over the forest's page; catx.py reads the public catalog without a key); the federal:usfs row covers the generic agency rules. ACCESS: never measure grades; act only on what the agency or operator says (a 4WD/high-clearance requirement or recommendation, or 'motorhomes/trailers not recommended', is a drop candidate - quote it). LOCAL: the price gate does not apply, but give the current RV rate and source, who runs it, how it is booked (Keene's RecDesk engine), hookups and amps, season, site count and max length. PRIVATE: price for a 23-ft motorhome (base rate under US$50 in ANY season - shoulder-season and weekday nights count; or a gateway within 40 km via audit/price_research/gateways.py; a Good Sam Park at 10% off or an operator-posted member discount also passes; give the rate, date and source), Google rating and count (under 4.0 with 25+ reviews = drop candidate), RV Life rating and price tier where stored, the price and presence indicators, and the five long-stay tests. New England private campgrounds are often seasonal-heavy (season-long sites sold by the summer, nightly sites a minority): measure the nightly share from the site map or engine and quote the wording; a park that mostly sells seasons AND looks iffy is a drop candidate. Holiday-weekend 3-night minimums are time-of-year minimums and stay; a standing 3+ night minimum all summer is a drop candidate. Clearance: a posted height limit of 10.5 ft or more passes. WATERFRONT (confirm, log a lead, or recommend a downgrade; never upgrade without named sites): New Hampshire has lakes, ponds, rivers and a short seacoast. Lakefront/riverfront/creekside/pond need named sites within about 50 m over open ground a camper can walk across to the water (the kayak test: pads 1-2 m above the edge over a gentle bank pass; a 2-3.7 m bank at ~30 deg is a view; creekside needs only an easy walk down). A river or channel under ~30 m wide is creekside. White Mountains campgrounds sit under continuous canopy: satellite can only veto - use the FS or state per-site map and rec.gov/ReserveAmerica per-site photos to place riverside sites, and leaf-off imagery (Esri Wayback, NH GRANIT orthos) where canopy hides the river. Hampton Beach State Park's RV campground is stored `coastal dunes`: check it hard against the curation doc's coastal-dunes rule (sites on open sand/dune to the ocean, no road or seawall between) and say what lies between the RV pads and the beach; `coastal walk` is the alternative where the ocean is an easy walk (up to about a mile) with no highway, town or residential strip between. Pins often sit at a park office, entrance or day-use beach: read every pin against Esri imagery and report any more than about 250 m off the camping loops with corrected coordinates.
