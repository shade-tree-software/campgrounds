The twelve are all WYOMING (US; US dollars, the US$50 price gate for private parks). This is the first Wyoming round of phase 7 (the state was swept 2026-07-02, ids 6349-6570, plus later adds). Wyoming batches are mostly FEDERAL: national forest campgrounds (Shoshone, Bridger-Teton, Bighorn, Medicine Bow-Routt, Black Hills, Caribou-Targhee, Ashley/Flaming Gorge NRA), BLM campgrounds, and the National Park Service (Yellowstone, Grand Teton, Bighorn Canyon NRA, Devils Tower); then Wyoming State Parks, town/county campgrounds and fairgrounds (local), and private parks. OWNERSHIP: `state` = Wyoming State Parks (wyoparks.wyo.gov), including the Reclamation reservoirs the state runs (Boysen, Buffalo Bill, Glendo, Guernsey, Keyhole, Seminoe, Hawk Springs) and Game and Fish designated camping areas; `federal` = USFS, BLM, NPS, Reclamation-run sites; `local` = town, city and county campgrounds and county fairgrounds; `private` = commercial parks, including concession-free private resorts outside the parks. Report any entry whose stored ownership breaks this (the sweep found RV Life tagging BLM sites `dnr` and NPS/fairground sites `commercial`). STATE PARKS: the state:WY policy row in /home/andrew/Dev/campgrounds/campground_policies.json already holds the reserve.wyoming.gov (usedirect) booking, the 120/113-day window, the 14-in-30 stay limit, first-come outside the season, the $10/$18 base camping fee, the $7/$12 day-use fee, the $10 electric add-on, the reservation fee and tax - do not write any of that per entry, and strip booking/fee/permit boilerplate from notes (the fields show it). Per state-park entry confirm from the park's wyoparks.wyo.gov page and its reserve.wyoming.gov listing: which campground(s) the entry covers (whole-park entries such as Glendo, Boysen, Buffalo Bill and Keyhole hold several campgrounds - measure each and name the one the pin and evidence describe), which sites have electric (and amps), water/sewer, the site count, dump station, showers, reservable versus first-come sites, the 2026 season (and any winter camping), and the per-site maximum equipment length (report how many sites take 23 ft+; a uniform value on every site may be a placeholder). FEDERAL: rec.gov / RIDB campsite catalog for fit, hookups, fees, season and first-come vs reservable (trust rec.gov over older notes and over the forest's own page; catx.py reads the public catalog without a key); the federal:usfs / federal:blm / federal:nps / federal:bor rows cover the generic agency rules. A forest or BLM campground with no recreation.gov listing is first-come only (AWH 2026-10-22, the Montana round). Small free or cheap primitive forest and BLM campgrounds stay on little evidence unless reports say closed or locked. Grizzly-country food-storage orders are forest-wide boilerplate: do not write them per entry (a campground restricted to hard-sided units does not exclude a motorhome). ACCESS: never measure grades; an agency or operator statement that a road needs 4WD or high clearance (required or recommended), or that motorhomes/trailers are not recommended, is a drop candidate - quote it; a "steep" or "rough" road with no rig restriction is not, and where the agency names a route for campers, say which. A per-site or campground-wide length cap under ~23 ft is a drop candidate. NPS: Yellowstone's concession campgrounds (Bridge Bay, Canyon, Grant Village, Madison, Fishing Bridge RV Park) book through Yellowstone National Park Lodges (platform operator); the NPS-run ones and Grand Teton's book on recreation.gov - confirm each from the park's own campground page (nps.gov) and give the 2026 season, any 2026 closure or construction, site count, length limits and the generator rules only if they bar a 23-ft rig; the price gate does not apply to federal campgrounds. LOCAL (town/county/fairgrounds): the price gate does not apply, but give the current RV rate and source, who runs it, how it is booked (online engine, phone, first-come honor box), hookups and amps, season, site count and max length, and whether it is a real public campground: many small-town Wyoming parks offer free or donation camping with a few electric posts - confirm designated campsites (not a parking-lot allowance) a 23-ft rig fits; a county fairground must be shown to rent ordinary nights outside events; a public campground with no agency or town page is kept when Google Maps lists it as a campground with a phone (the note then says to call that number to confirm it is open) or a recent Google review confirms overnight camping. PRIVATE: price for a 23-ft motorhome (base rate under US$50, or a gateway within 40 km via audit/price_research/gateways.py - Yellowstone and Grand Teton count, so Jackson and the park-edge resorts usually pass; Cody does not - a Good Sam Park at 10% off, or an operator-posted member discount; give the rate, date and source), Google rating and count (under 4.0 with 25+ reviews = drop candidate), RV Life rating and price tier where stored, the price indicator and presence indicator, and the five long-stay tests. ENERGY-TOWN RV PARKS (Gillette, Rock Springs, Green River, Pinedale, Evanston, Douglas, Casper, Rawlins) are often workforce housing: a park that sells mainly monthly to crews, or whose nightly sale is token, is a drop candidate - measure the nightly share and quote the wording. 'No posted nightly rate' alone is not a drop for an ordinary private park with a working website + phone (or Google 25+ reviews + phone) and an RV Life $/$$ tier. Clearance: a posted height limit of 10.5 ft or more passes. WATERFRONT (confirm, log a lead, or recommend a downgrade; never upgrade without named sites): Wyoming is inland - lakefront/riverfront/creekside/pond and the view values only, never coastal values. Within about 50 m of the water with only open ground between (open sagebrush or grass counts as open ground); willows, a cottonwood or tree band, marsh, a road or a trail between = a view value; a river or channel under ~30 m wide is creekside. The reservoirs (Glendo, Boysen, Buffalo Bill, Keyhole, Seminoe, Pathfinder, Alcova, Flaming Gorge, Bighorn Lake) draw down hard: measure to the high-water edge and say what lies between, with site numbers. Read the pin against Esri imagery for all twelve and report one more than about 250 m off the campsites with corrected coordinates (pins in earlier rounds sat on offices, day-use lots, trailheads and the wrong lake).
