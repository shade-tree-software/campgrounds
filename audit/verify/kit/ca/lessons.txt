Lessons from recent batches (IL/VT/WI/SK and the Montana round): booking.fcfs values are always / after_cutoff (reservations close N days or hours before arrival and open sites then go walk-up - set reserve_until too) / some_sites / never; nothing else. fees.extra_person is an object. Some phones are stored as an empty string: give new phones as empty -> new. Check that stored inclusion_evidence, note and links describe THIS campground (a Wisconsin entry carried a Washington park's data; Saskatchewan pins and evidence described a neighbouring private park or cottage subdivision); if not, give replacement text. Whole-park entries' stored evidence often has wrong counts and loop names: measure every campground in the park. Several stored names are wrong or out of date: give the agency's current name. An --AWH waterfront value is one signed --AWH in note or evidence. Season dates: give the 2027 date where it differs (2027 dates are rarely out in November: write 2026 and say so); a reservation window is not a season; a pool's or rink's season on a town page is not the campground's. Pins often sit at an office, entrance, gate, trailhead or day-use lot, and sometimes on the wrong lake: place the camping loops. For any private, town or county park that looks seasonal, measure the nightly share and give the Google rating + count; when kept at 10-50% nightly, the note says so plainly ("Mostly seasonal: about N of its M sites are sold by the night"). A park whose only posted rate is seasonal or monthly fails test 2; under 1 site in 10 nightly fails test 4; a park that sells or leases lots fails test 5 (a resort that also sells titled RV lots: quote the lot wording and measure the rental campground separately); a private park with no price anywhere and only a Facebook/directory page is a drop candidate; a mostly-seasonal private park needs a posted nightly rate or a booking site; but 'no posted nightly rate' is not a drop for an ordinary private park with presence and an RV Life $/$$ tier (Montana lesson: two parks were wrongly dropped on it). Price gate: the test is the cheapest real RV night (an electric site a 23-ft rig can book) in ANY season - a park whose spring/fall or weekday RV nights are $50 or under passes even if summer weekends are higher (AWH rulings 2026-10-30, 2026-11-16). No-hookup or tent-only sites don't count as the RV night. Report the summer peak too; a park that passes only narrowly and has mediocre Google reviews (under ~4.5) is a drop candidate for the reviewer, not an automatic drop. A booking engine's terms checkbox is not to be ticked: report the nightly share as unmeasured. Casino RV parking along a casino lot or truck-stop lot is not a campground. A fair association's campground stays local. A general campground with ATV trail access stays (just say so); an off-road park or OHV staging camp does not. Horse rule (any owner): horse sites mixed into the general loops with no separate horse area are a drop candidate; a mere amenity line or a single sold horse site in a general campground is not; a horse camp beside a general campground with its own loop is not the entry. A source that refuses bots is not dead: keep its link unless it is actually gone. A regional or town park can be long closed while directories still list it: check news and the agency's own list. When a stored count, rig length or amperage has no source, say so and give the sourced value if you found one. Never copy personal names (hosts, owners) from booking-site or review text. From the Montana round (federal-heavy, the nearest precedent): a forest/BLM campground with no recreation.gov listing is first-come only; "motorhomes not recommended", "trailers not recommended", "very tight", "limited RV access" or a high-clearance recommendation on the access road were drops; a "steep access road" with no rig restriction was kept, and an agency-named camper route (from one direction) with no clearance warning was kept with the route in the note; a day-use site where camping means the parking lot is not a campground; walk-in-only sites are not drive-in; closure orders and group-only campgrounds are out; a public campground with no agency page was kept on a Google Maps listing with a phone (call-first note) or a recent review confirming camping. A Google rating shared with a bar, motel, golf course or attraction is not the campground's alone - say so. Never run git, even read-only.
