The twelve are all MINNESOTA (US; US dollars, the US$50 price gate for private parks). Minnesota batches mix owners: county and city campgrounds (local), Minnesota DNR state parks and recreation areas, DNR state forest campgrounds, Chippewa and Superior National Forest campgrounds (federal, mostly recreation.gov), and private parks. STATE PARKS and RECREATION AREAS: the state:MN policy row in /home/andrew/Dev/campgrounds/campground_policies.json already holds the reservation-only rule (no first-come sites, same-day online booking works), the 120-day window, the 14-night limit, the $8/$10 reservation fee, the $7 vehicle permit, the $10 electric / $8 water-sewer add-ons and sales tax - do not write any of that per entry, and strip booking/permit boilerplate from notes (the fields show it). Per state park confirm from the park's page on dnr.state.mn.us and the reservation listing (reservemn.usedirect.com / Minnesota State Parks reservations): the nightly price for a drive-in site (each park's own price, $20-$35), which sites have electric (and amps) or water/sewer, the site count, dump station, showers/flush toilets, season (record the published 2026/2027 dates; many loops close or go rustic in winter), and the per-site maximum equipment length the reservation system posts (report how many sites take 23 ft+; a campground-wide cap under ~23 ft is a drop candidate; a uniform value on every site may be a placeholder - say so, and give the spread where it varies). STATE FORESTS are a separate system NOT covered by that row: they keep first-come sites and their fees include tax - record what the DNR state forest page says. FEDERAL: rec.gov / RIDB campsite catalog for fit, hookups, fees, season, first-come vs reservable (trust rec.gov over older notes and over the forest's own page); the federal:usfs row covers generic USFS rules. LOCAL (county/city): the price gate does not apply, but give the current RV rate and source, who runs it, how it is booked (online engine, phone, first-come), hookups, season, and whether it is a real public campground rather than a seasonal-lease park (a city park whose sites are mostly rented by the season, with a few nightly sites, faces the long-stay five tests like a private park); Google rating and count where the operator is a concessionaire. PRIVATE: price for a 23-ft motorhome (base rate under US$50, or a gateway within 40 km - Minneapolis-St Paul counts - a Good Sam Park at 10% off, or an operator-posted member discount; give the rate, date and source), Google rating and count (under 4.0 with 25+ reviews on any site = drop candidate), RV Life rating where shown, and the long-stay five tests (mostly seasonal, nights sold, occupancy - Minnesota lake-country private parks are often seasonal-heavy, so check how many sites sell nights). Clearance: a posted height limit of 10.5 ft or more passes. Waterfront: Minnesota's many lake campgrounds were swept with generous values; check each stored waterfront value on imagery and downgrade where the sites are not on open ground within ~50 m of the water (behind a tree band = a view value); an upgrade or a caveat needs site numbers placed on imagery (the campground map or reservation map numbers them).
