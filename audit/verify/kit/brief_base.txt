Rules that matter most:
- Write only what a page actually states, with the page's URL as `source`. Never a guess or a typical value. Silence leaves a field alone. A `derived` value that rests only on an aggregator/directory and that the operator's pages don't state: clear it with null and say so.
- The fees base rate is the cheapest site a 23-ft motorhome fits (not tent-only/primitive, and not a loop restricted to shorter units). Montana State Parks charge residents and nonresidents differently (and residents with the vehicle registration fee may get discounts): record what the official fee page states, base = the resident rate, nonresident in the non-resident field, per README.
- Inherited agency values: confirm into the entry only if THIS campground's page states the value; write the campground's own value if it differs; leave it alone if silent. Flag an inherited row that looks wrong.
- Never overwrite a value whose provenance method is manual/reported.
- Clean the note on every entry you change (README step 7): drop text, --AWH included, that only repeats a structured value or that the pages contradict, listing dropped --AWH runs in `awh_superseded`. Keep what no field can say. Never add structured facts to a note.
- A standing minimum stay (2+ nights outside weekends/holidays): record it in booking.min_stay. recreation.gov minimums marked `soft` (value 2+) ARE rules: record them in booking.min_stay and say in booking.note that they may be relaxed for a same-day booking. A peak-season long-stay minimum is acceptable only if it lasts 3 months or less - record it, keep a "PEAK-SEASON MINIMUM: ..." line in the proposed note, and flag anything longer.
- Federal entries: recreation.gov API + the agency's own page (fs.usda.gov/r01/..., nps.gov, usbr.gov, blm.gov). Say who operates each (a concessionaire on federal land is still `federal` ownership, but name it) and whether it is FCFS. Check current Forest Service alerts/closure orders for the campground and its access road; a road closure is a flag, not a status, unless the campground itself is closed. NPS: developer.nps.gov/api/v1/campgrounds?parkCode=XXXX&api_key=DEMO_KEY and /alerts. Montana season statements matter a lot (many USFS campgrounds open late May - mid Sept): only a statement about THE CAMPGROUND counts.
- Dispersed/boondocking entries: find the agency page that designates the sites; check they are drive-in sites a 23-ft motorhome can reach on the stated road. EKKO is NOT 4WD: an agency 4WD or high-clearance requirement OR recommendation is a criteria flag. Flag an entry with no agency page at all.
- Private campgrounds: operator's own website or its booking engine; rates need a 2024+ date. US$50 gate (flag only; nearest gateway from `python3 -I /home/user/campgrounds/audit/price_research/gateways.py lat,lng`); booking/reservation fees charged once per stay are not counted, mandatory per-night fees are. The presence rule (Facebook-only, dead or parked site, unclaimed RoverPass, Airbnb/Hipcamp-only listing with no own site = flag; AWH 2026-10-14: a working website with a phone, or Google Maps open with 25+ reviews and a phone). The long-stay and workforce tests (seasonal-only sites, homes or lots for sale, no posted nightly rate, monthly-only, short stays by approval; memory feedback_exclude_seasonal_residential: nights must really be for sale to a passing traveller). Also flag any posted minimum rig length over 23 ft and any single-occupancy-only rule.
- A current closure banner (storm, flood, fire, construction) is recorded as status temporarily_closed (as results/applied/197.json does) with the banner quoted, and flagged.
- Contrary operator/booking-engine water claims (either direction): flag as a waterfront re-audit lead (do not change `waterfront`). Also flag a wrong/missing phone (give the official number), wrong ownership class, wrong name vs the agency's/booking site's published name, missing operator/agency website (give the URL), dead/parked/stale/compromised website, a website belonging to a different campground, or an aggregator link (thedyrt, campendium, airbnb) in `website`.
- Public campground with no agency or town page (AWH 2026-10-22): an official tourism website (a state tourism office such as visitmt.com, or a government-designated tourism region / county or city visitor bureau - not a commercial directory) that describes the campground in detail stands in as its official page - give its URL as the website (a flag) and cite it as `source` with method `reported`. If neither an agency page nor an official tourism site covers it, check Google Maps under the browser lock (https://www.google.com/maps/search/<name+town>): report whether it is listed as a campground, its review count, its phone, and any review from the last two years that confirms overnight camping. A public entry listed as a campground with a phone, or with a recent review confirming camping, is kept (a note asks travellers to call first); flag it only if Google shows neither. Club- or nonprofit-run parks fall under the private presence rule; dispersed areas still need an agency designation.
- Rig length from reviews (AWH 2026-10-22): a reviewer's rig length never goes into sites.max_rig_ft (one rig fitting is not a limit). Where the agency gives NO length, look for a review from the last 3 years in which the reviewer says their OWN rig of 23 ft or more fit (with its length and type); if found, propose it as a dated, attributed note caveat, e.g. 'A 2025 Campendium reviewer reports fitting a 30-ft trailer; the Forest Service gives no length. --Claude'. Where the agency is VAGUE ('small trailers only', 'limited access for RVs'), such a review settles it as a keep; with none, flag it as a criteria problem. Where the agency is EXPLICIT ('motorhomes/large RVs not recommended', 4WD or high clearance), flag it whatever reviews say - EXCEPT that a 'large RVs not recommended' line is not a problem when the same agency also states a site/trailer length of 23 ft or more (AWH 2026-10-22: 23 ft isn't 'large' there); a 4WD/high-clearance statement is always a flag.
