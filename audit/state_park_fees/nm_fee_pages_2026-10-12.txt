Camping Fee and rule changes Q. Is the campground checkout time changing? A. Checkout time has traditionally been 2:00 PM. However, parks now have the authority to post a different check-out time due to staffing needs that may be earlier or later. The time must be posted. Q. If campsites share a water spigot, instead of having a spigot at each site, will the water fee still be charged per site? A. No. The water charge is for sites where it's provided exclusively for the use of that one site. Sites that share utilities would not be charged utility fees. Q. Can a visitor with an Annual Camping Pass/Permit use a park’s dump station whether they camped at the park or not? A. Yes. Visitors with Annual Camping Passes/Permits may use the park’s dump station without payment of the dump station fee. Q. If I have an Annual Camping Pass/Permit and I use a site with utility hookups, do I still have to pay the fee for the utilities. A. Yes. The Annual Camping Pass/Permit covers the daily cost of developed camping but does not include utility fees. Q. How many consecutive days can a visitor camp in a State Park campground? A. Stay limits in our campgrounds will change in 2025 to accommodate more visitors in our busy season. The busy season for most State Parks is May 1 through September 30. Visitors may camp for a maximum of 7 days out of any 20-calendar day period from May 1 through September 30th. In the off season, October 1 through April 30, visitors may camp for a maximum of 14 days out of any 20-calendar day period.
